# JET Programme (Japan Exchange and Teaching)

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** FC-001

## Overview

The JET Programme is a Japanese government initiative that places native English speakers in schools and government offices across Japan. Running since 1987, it's one of the most established and best-paid teaching abroad programs in the world.

**Important for young adults:** JET requires a completed bachelor's degree by the departure date. If the reader is under 21 without a degree, he is not currently eligible. This is a firm requirement with no exceptions. However, this document is valuable for future planning—JET is worth pursuing after completing any four-year degree in any field.

## What You'll Do

### ALT (Assistant Language Teacher) — Most Common

You work alongside Japanese teachers of English (JTEs) in public elementary, junior high, or high schools. You assist with English instruction—not lead classes independently.

**Day-to-day reality:**
- Co-teach with a Japanese teacher. Your role varies by JTE. Some want active involvement; others want you to read vocabulary aloud and nothing more.
- Significant desk time between classes ("desk warming"). During exam periods, you may sit at your desk with no teaching duties.
- May visit multiple schools or be placed at a single school.

### CIR (Coordinator for International Relations)

Work in local government offices doing translation, interpretation, and international exchange activities.

**Requirement:** Functional Japanese (approximately JLPT N2 level). Not for entry-level Japanese learners.

### SEA (Sports Exchange Advisor)

Coach or assist with sports programs. Very few positions available.

## Requirements

- **Bachelor's Degree:** Officially conferred by June 26th, 2026 (for 2026 departures). Any field accepted. No exceptions. Completing coursework is not sufficient—the degree must be formally awarded.
- **US Citizenship:** Must be a US citizen by application deadline.
- **No Criminal Record:** Cannot have a record that would prevent visa issuance.
- **Age:** No age limit if other requirements are met.
- **Teaching Experience:** Not required for ALT positions.
- **Japanese Language:** Not required for ALT (though it helps significantly).

## Training Provided

- Pre-Departure Orientation in your home country
- 2-3 day Tokyo Orientation upon arrival
- Ongoing support through prefectural advisors and AJET community
- No formal teaching credential results, but valuable classroom experience

## The Money Reality

### Pay (Effective April 2025)

| Year | Annual Salary | Monthly (Pre-Tax) |
|------|--------------|-------------------|
| 1st year | ¥4,020,000 | ¥335,000 |
| 2nd year | ¥4,140,000 | ¥345,000 |
| 3rd year | ¥4,260,000 | ¥355,000 |
| 4th-5th year | ¥4,320,000 | ¥360,000 |

At ~¥150 = $1 USD, first-year salary is approximately $26,800-$27,000 USD/year.

### Deductions

Approximately ¥41,000/month for:
- Employment insurance
- Health insurance (Shakai Hoken)
- National pension

**Take-home pay:** ~¥280,000-¥295,000/month first year.

### What's Provided

- Round-trip flights (to Japan and return upon completing contract)
- Health insurance
- 10-20 days paid vacation per year
- Housing arranged by contracting organization (rent subsidized, typically ¥20,000-¥75,000/month)

### Startup Costs

**Critical:** You need savings before earning.

**Recommended:** $2,500-$3,000 USD minimum

Initial costs include:
- Security deposit (1-3 months' rent)
- Key money (1-2 months' rent, non-refundable)
- First month's rent
- Utilities setup
- Basic furnishings
- Cell phone setup

Your first paycheck arrives 4-6 weeks after arrival. Plan accordingly.

### Savings Potential

With disciplined budgeting, many JETs save ¥100,000-¥120,000/month ($650-$800), especially in rural areas.

## How to Get Started

### Application Timeline (2026 Departure)

1. **September-October 2025:** Application opens
2. **Mid-November 2025:** Application deadline
3. **January 2026:** Interview notifications
4. **Late January-February 2026:** In-person interviews at Japanese consulates
5. **April 2026:** Results announced
6. **May 2026:** Placement notifications
7. **Late July-August 2026:** Departure

### Steps

1. **Confirm eligibility.** Bachelor's degree must be officially conferred by June 26th, 2026.

2. **Create account at jetprogramusa.org** when applications open.

3. **Prepare materials:**
   - Personal statement
   - Two recommendation letters
   - Official transcripts
   - Passport photo
   - Resume

4. **Submit before deadline.** Late applications not accepted.

5. **Prepare for interview** if selected.

6. **Accept placement.** You cannot choose your location—JET assigns based on program needs.

## The Hard Parts

### The Placement Lottery

You do not choose where you go. Many JETs end up in small towns or rural villages hours from major cities—being the only foreigner in town, requiring a car, with limited entertainment options.

### Isolation and Mental Health

Culture shock follows a predictable cycle. Hardest around months 3-6. Common challenges:
- Language barrier in daily life
- Social isolation, especially in rural areas
- Being visibly "different" everywhere
- Missing family and friends

### The Job Itself

Your experience depends entirely on your Japanese co-teachers. Some ALTs have collaborative JTEs; others are treated as human tape recorders. Desk warming is real. Professional autonomy is limited.

### Limited Career Progression

JET caps at 5 years maximum. No path to permanent employment. After JET, you must find new work. ALT experience doesn't automatically qualify you for credentialed teaching positions.

### Reverse Culture Shock

Many JETs struggle when returning home. The transition back can be harder than the transition to Japan.
