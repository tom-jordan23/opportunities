# Team Rubicon

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09

## Overview

Team Rubicon is a veteran-led disaster response organization that deploys volunteers ("Greyshirts") to help communities recover from disasters. Despite its military roots, **30-40% of volunteers are civilians** with no military or first responder background.

For young adults, this offers hands-on disaster response experience, practical skills training (including chainsaw certification), and a strong network—all at no cost. The catch: it's unpaid volunteer work, so he'd need another income source.

## What You'll Do

Team Rubicon responds to natural disasters and humanitarian crises with three main operation types:

**Immediate Response**
- Damage assessment and needs evaluation
- Search and rescue support
- Emergency supply distribution

**Recovery Operations**
- Mucking out flooded homes (removing debris, damaged materials)
- Tarping damaged roofs
- Chainsaw work (clearing fallen trees)
- Debris removal

**Rebuild Operations**
- Home repairs and reconstruction
- Community rebuilding projects
- Longer-term recovery support

Deployments typically last 4-7 days for domestic operations. Local operations can be single-day commitments.

## Requirements

- **Age:** 18+ for most operations (16+ with parental waiver for some local events)
- **Citizenship:** Not required for domestic operations
- **Background Check:** Required (processed through Roll Call platform, $19-35 upfront fee reimbursed after completing TR101 training or first operation)
- **Physical:** Ability to perform manual labor in challenging conditions
- **Skills:** None required—only prerequisite is "a commitment to service"
- **Time:** Completely flexible. No minimum hours required.

## Training Provided

All training is free:

**Core Training**
- TR101 online course (required before first deployment)
- Basic disaster response procedures
- Safety protocols

**Specialized Certifications**
- Sawyer Program: Chainsaw operation certification (Level 1 and Level 2 available, with instructor track)
- Heavy equipment training through CASE Construction Equipment partnership (compact track loaders and excavators)
- ICS (Incident Command System) certifications (ICS-200 and ICS-300 for leadership roles)
- Leadership development tracks

**Annual Conference**
- Training summit for 500+ volunteers
- Advanced skills development
- Networking opportunities

## The Hard Parts

**It's unpaid.** You need income from somewhere else. Deployments mean taking time off work.

**Physically demanding.** Mucking flooded homes, chainsaw work, and debris removal in harsh conditions. Not for everyone.

**Emotionally heavy.** You're working with people on their worst days. Homes destroyed, lives disrupted. Secondary traumatic stress is real.

**Veteran-centric culture.** 60-70% of volunteers are veterans or first responders. Military terminology and structure. Civilians report feeling welcomed but note the cultural adjustment.

**Unpredictable timing.** Disasters don't schedule themselves. Response operations may have 48-hour deployment notice.

**Organizational growing pains.** Glassdoor reviews note internal challenges—layoffs reduced staff from 200 to approximately 140 (2024-2025). Some employee reviews cite management and culture concerns, though this primarily affects paid staff rather than volunteers.
