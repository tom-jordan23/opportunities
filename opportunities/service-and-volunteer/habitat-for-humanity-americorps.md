# Habitat for Humanity AmeriCorps

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** FC-001

## Overview

Habitat for Humanity partners with AmeriCorps to place service members at local Habitat affiliates across the United States. You'll help build affordable homes while earning a living allowance and education award.

For young adults, this combines construction skill-building with structured service and actual compensation—unlike pure volunteer programs. The pay is modest (below minimum wage when calculated hourly), but housing assistance is sometimes available, and the education award can fund trade school or pay off student loans.

## What You'll Do

Roles vary by affiliate, but common positions include:

**Construction Crew Member/Leader**
- Hands-on home building (framing, roofing, siding, drywall, painting)
- Leading groups of community volunteers
- Teaching construction basics to homebuyers and volunteers
- Site preparation and cleanup

**Volunteer Services Coordinator**
- Recruiting and scheduling volunteers
- Managing volunteer groups on build sites
- Organizing corporate and community build days
- Volunteer recognition and retention

**ReStore Operations**
- Working in Habitat ReStores (thrift stores that fund builds)
- Donor outreach and pickup coordination
- E-commerce and online sales
- Store operations and customer service

**Community Outreach/Family Services**
- Homebuyer education and support
- Community partnership development
- Program administration

## Requirements

- **Age:** 18+ (no upper limit)
- **Citizenship:** U.S. citizen, national, or lawful permanent resident
- **Education:** High school diploma or GED
- **Background Check:** Required (AmeriCorps National Service Criminal History Check)
- **Physical:** For construction roles—ability to lift 50 lbs, work on feet, work at heights, use power tools
- **Time:** Full-time commitment (10.5-12 months, 1,700 hours)
- **Prior Experience:** Not required

## Training Provided

**Construction Skills**
- Power tool operation (circular saws, nail guns, drills)
- Framing, roofing, siding installation
- Drywall hanging and finishing
- Basic plumbing and electrical concepts
- Safety protocols (OSHA 10 often included)

**Leadership Development**
- Volunteer management
- Project coordination
- AmeriCorps-wide training opportunities

**Professional Skills**
- Nonprofit operations
- Community engagement
- Time management and accountability

No formal trade certifications, but practical experience is valuable for apprenticeship applications.

## The Money Reality

### Living Allowance

| Program Type | Total Allowance | Monthly Equivalent |
|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| AmeriCorps State/National | $20,400-$25,500 | $1,700-$2,125 |
| AmeriCorps NCCC (team-based) | ~$360-380/month + housing | ~$360-380 |

**Important:** This is a stipend, not wages. Calculated hourly, it's below minimum wage. Many AmeriCorps members qualify for SNAP (food stamps).

### Education Award

Upon completing full-time service:
- **Segal AmeriCorps Education Award:** $7,395 (tied to Pell Grant maximum, may change annually)
- Can be used for college tuition, trade school, or student loan payments
- Expires 7 years after earning
- **Note:** Unlike AmeriCorps VISTA, State/National members (including Habitat) do not have a cash stipend alternative

### Benefits

- Health insurance at no premium for full-time members
- Possible childcare assistance
- Student loan forbearance during service
- Some affiliates provide housing assistance (varies widely—ask during interview)

### What You Pay

- Housing (unless affiliate provides it—many don't)
- Food (SNAP eligibility helps)
- Transportation to work
- Personal expenses

### The Math

At 1,700 hours and $24,000 total allowance: ~$14.12/hour equivalent. With $7,395 education award counted: ~$18.47/hour total value. Still modest, but better than unpaid volunteering.

## How to Get Started

1. **Search positions at my.americorps.gov** — Filter by "Habitat" or browse construction/housing categories

2. **Check Habitat affiliate websites directly** — Local affiliates post positions on their own sites

3. **Apply to multiple positions** — Affiliate quality varies significantly; cast a wide net

4. **Ask about housing in interviews** — Some affiliates help with housing; many don't. Clarify before accepting.

5. **Complete AmeriCorps enrollment** — Background check, paperwork, orientation

6. **Start service** — Programs have rolling start dates

## The Hard Parts

**The pay is poverty-level.** $2,000/month doesn't go far, especially without housing assistance. Many members qualify for SNAP and struggle financially.

**Affiliate quality varies dramatically.** Over 1,200 Habitat affiliates exist, and experiences differ wildly. Some are well-organized with great support; others are chaotic. Research specific affiliates before accepting.

**Physically demanding.** Construction work in all weather—heat, cold, rain. Lifting, climbing, standing all day. If you're not ready for physical labor, this isn't for you.

**You're temporary.** As an AmeriCorps member, you're not staff. Limited voice in organizational decisions, no long-term job security.

**Government bureaucracy.** AmeriCorps paperwork, time tracking, and reporting requirements add administrative burden.

**Housing is NOT guaranteed.** Unlike AmeriCorps NCCC, most Habitat State/National positions don't include housing. You find your own.

## Where This Can Lead

**Immediate Outcomes (from Habitat/AmeriCorps data):**
- 76% gain homebuilding experience
- 75% learn volunteer management
- 41% pursue four-year degrees within 3 years

**Career Paths:**
- Construction trades (use education award for apprenticeship programs)
- Nonprofit management
- Community development
- Habitat staff positions (some members get hired)

**The Education Award** is flexible:
- Trade school tuition (electrical, plumbing, HVAC programs)
- Community college
- Student loan payments
- Vocational certifications
