# City Year AmeriCorps

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** Claude Opus 4.5

## Overview

City Year is an education-focused AmeriCorps program where you serve as a tutor, mentor, and role model in high-need urban public schools. You work full-time for 11 months alongside teachers. You help students develop academic skills and confidence. You run after-school programs. You earn a modest stipend and an education award upon completion.

This program works for Sam because it provides a structured entry into education and youth development work. You do not need a college degree. You do not need teaching experience. You get health insurance. You earn an education award worth $7,395 that can pay for future training or reduce student loans.

The catch: the stipend is poverty-level wages. You work 10-hour days in under-resourced schools. The emotional weight of working with struggling students is real. You serve in urban areas where the cost of living exceeds your pay. Many corps members need second jobs or family support to get through the year.

## What You'll Do

Your day starts before students arrive. You stand at the school entrance greeting students as they walk in. This sounds simple. It sets the tone for the day. Students who feel welcomed show up more often.

During classes, you rotate through the classroom. You sit with students who need extra help. You pull students aside for one-on-one tutoring or small group instruction. You help with math, reading, and other core subjects. You track attendance. You call families when students miss school. You work closely with your partner teacher to identify students who are falling behind.

Between classes and during lunch, you connect with students. You build relationships. You become someone students trust.

After school ends, you run clubs and enrichment programs. These might include robotics, coding, performing arts, sports, or whatever interests students and your team can deliver.

During school breaks and teacher prep days, you go to the local City Year office. You attend training sessions. You work on professional development. You build team cohesion.

A typical day runs 10 hours, four days a week. The fifth day is shorter. You work school hours plus before-school arrival and after-school programs.

## Requirements

- **Age:** 18-25 years old at start of service (as of July 1 of the service year).
- **Citizenship:** U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident. DACA recipients are not eligible for AmeriCorps service.
- **Education:** High school diploma or GED required.
- **Background check:** Required. Includes FBI fingerprint check, national sex offender search, and state searches in your current residence and service state. Being on the sex offender registry or having a murder conviction disqualifies you. Other criminal history is reviewed case by case.
- **Prior AmeriCorps service:** If you have completed four terms in any AmeriCorps State or National program, you are not eligible.
- **Physical requirements:** You need to be on your feet most of the day. You need energy to engage with students. No specific fitness test required.
- **Time commitment:** 11 months, full-time. Most members serve 1,700 hours to earn the full education award.
- **Flexibility required:** You go where you are placed within your chosen city.

## Training Provided

City Year provides 2-4 weeks of training before you enter schools. This is called Basic Training Academy. You learn City Year's approach to student support, the organization's culture and values, and practical classroom strategies.

Throughout the year, you get at least one full Learning and Development Day each quarter. These cover service skills, resume writing, public speaking, and career planning.

Midway through service, you attend a Mid-Year Summit. You reflect on your growth. You set goals for the second half of the year.

Training uses instructor-led sessions, online learning, and on-the-job coaching. Your team leader provides ongoing feedback.

**Credentials earned:** No formal industry certifications. You develop skills in tutoring, classroom management, youth mentoring, and program facilitation. These skills transfer to education, nonprofit, and people-focused careers.

## The Money Reality

### Pay

Stipends vary by location and service term. All amounts are biweekly and pre-tax.

**1700-hour service term (Student Success Coach):**
- Lowest: $907 biweekly (Columbia, SC and New Hampshire)
- Highest: $1,386 biweekly (Bay Area, CA)
- After taxes (roughly 17% federal and state): $753-$1,150 biweekly take-home

**To be direct:** At the lowest rate, you earn roughly $1,814 per month before taxes. In Columbia, SC, this might be manageable with a roommate. In the Bay Area at $2,772 monthly, you still cannot afford San Francisco rent. The stipend attempts to adjust for cost of living but falls short in expensive cities.

### Housing

City Year does not provide housing. This is a critical difference from NCCC.

City Year offers:
- Contact information for rental agencies in your service city
- Neighborhood recommendations
- Access to an online community to connect with other incoming members looking for roommates

You are responsible for finding and paying for housing. In cities like New York, Boston, Los Angeles, and the Bay Area, this is the biggest financial challenge. Many corps members share apartments with 3-4 roommates.

### Startup Costs

- **Relocation allowance:** $500 one-time payment if you move 50+ miles to your service site
- **Housing deposit:** First and last month's rent, typically $1,500-3,000 depending on city
- **Personal expenses before first stipend:** 2-4 weeks of living expenses

**Out of pocket:** Expect to need $2,000-4,000 saved before starting. This covers housing deposits and initial living costs before your first stipend arrives.

### Benefits

- **Segal Education Award:** $7,395 upon successful completion (1,700-hour term). Smaller awards for shorter service terms.
- **Health insurance:** Medical, dental, and vision coverage provided
- **Mental health support:** Employee Assistance Program, Talkspace therapy access, Ten Percent Happier meditation app (free 3-year membership)
- **Student loan forbearance:** Federal loans can be placed in forbearance during service. AmeriCorps pays the interest that accrues if you complete service.
- **Childcare assistance:** Available for 1,700-hour members with children
- **Time off:** Up to 12 pre-planned days off plus 11 recognized holidays
- **Localized benefits:** Some sites offer transit passes or local discounts

### Hidden Costs

- **Housing in expensive cities:** Your biggest expense. The stipend does not cover market-rate rent in most City Year locations.
- **Second job pressure:** Many corps members work evenings or weekends to make ends meet. This adds exhaustion to an already demanding schedule.
- **Professional clothing:** You need appropriate clothes for a school environment daily.
- **Career opportunity cost:** 11 months of below-minimum-wage work while peers build resumes and savings.

## How to Get Started

1. **Go to cityyear.org/apply-now** and create an account. The application takes about 45 minutes.

2. **Choose up to three preferred service locations.** Research cost of living carefully. Lower-cost cities like Memphis, Little Rock, or Tulsa stretch your stipend further than Boston or Los Angeles.

3. **Complete the application.** You need employment history, education background, two professional references, and answers to essay questions about why you want to serve.

4. **Submit and wait.** City Year reviews applications on a rolling basis. The process includes a phone or video interview.

5. **Complete background check and documentation.** You need to provide proof of citizenship or permanent residency, your original Social Security card, and complete the criminal background check process.

6. **Attend Registration Day.** This happens before your service begins. Bring required documentation.

7. **Arrange housing.** Start looking for roommates through City Year's online community as soon as you are accepted. Do not wait until the last minute.

8. **Show up for Basic Training Academy.** Service begins in late July or early August, following your service city's school calendar.

## The Hard Parts

**The money does not work in most cities.** Corps members consistently report the stipend is not enough to live on. In expensive cities, many take second jobs, rely on family support, or go into debt. If you have bills, dependents, or no savings cushion, the financial stress will compound everything else.

**The work is emotionally demanding.** You work with students facing poverty, trauma, family instability, and educational gaps. Some students will not respond to your help. Some will face crises you cannot fix. Burnout is common. Mental health struggles among corps members are widely reported in reviews.

**The hours are long.** Ten-hour days at school. Training and meetings on top of that. After working with students all day, you may be expected to attend team events or organization activities. Work-life balance is rare during the service year.

**The "culture" can feel forced.** City Year has a strong organizational culture with chants, traditions, and team activities. Some people love this. Others find it performative or exhausting after long days. Reviews frequently mention pressure to participate in unpaid activities outside work hours.

**Training may not match reality.** Multiple reviews mention being thrown into schools with minimal preparation. Your actual experience depends heavily on your team leader, partner teacher, and school placement. Quality varies significantly across sites.

**You cannot choose your exact placement.** You pick a city, not a school. Your school assignment may not match your preferences or strengths.

**Review ratings have declined.** Glassdoor shows only 52% of corps members would recommend City Year to a friend. This suggests systemic challenges beyond individual experience variation.

## Where This Can Lead

### Direct Benefits

**Education Award:** The $7,395 Segal Education Award can pay for:
- Future college or trade school tuition
- Qualified student loan payments
- Educational travel abroad

You have seven years to use the award. Over 100 colleges and universities offer matching scholarships or additional support for City Year alumni, with some offering up to 100% tuition scholarships.

**Skills gained:**
- Classroom management and tutoring
- Youth mentoring and relationship building
- Communication and public speaking
- Cultural competency and equity awareness
- Program planning and facilitation

### Career Pathways

**Education sector:** Nearly half of City Year alumni work in education. An estimated 6,000 alumni are classroom teachers. City Year partners with teaching residency programs including Teach For America, Boston Teacher Residency, and NYC Teacher Residency. If you want to become a teacher, this is a strong entry point.

**Nonprofit and social services:** Alumni move into youth development organizations, community nonprofits, and social work. Organizations like Reading Partners, iMentor, Year Up, and Playworks actively recruit City Year alumni.

**Employers of National Service:** Over 600 organizations have signed up to preferentially hire AmeriCorps alumni. This includes nonprofits, government agencies, and some private sector employers.

**Graduate school:** The education award helps with tuition. The experience strengthens applications for education, social work, public policy, and related programs.

### Alumni Network

City Year has over 40,000 alumni. The LinkedIn alumni group has 6,000+ members. Alumni report:
- 84% say City Year helped prepare them for their career
- 91% say City Year had a positive impact on their life and career

This network is strongest for careers in education and social impact sectors.
