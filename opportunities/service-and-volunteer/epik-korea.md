# EPIK (English Program in Korea)

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** FC-001

## Critical Requirement: Bachelor's Degree

**Note: This program requires a bachelor's degree from an accredited university.** At 20, you likely do not have this yet. EPIK is not an option until you complete a four-year degree.

If you are determined to teach English abroad without a degree, some private academies in other countries may hire you, but South Korea's E-2 visa regulations legally require a bachelor's degree. There are no workarounds.

This document is included so you understand what the opportunity looks like if you pursue a degree later—or if you are reading this after completing one.

## Overview

EPIK is the English Program in Korea, a government-run initiative that places native English speakers in Korean public schools. The Korean Ministry of Education manages the program through the National Institute for International Education (NIIED).

Unlike teaching at private academies (hagwons), EPIK offers government employment with predictable hours, guaranteed pay, and strong job protections. You teach alongside Korean English teachers in elementary, middle, or high schools across the country.

The program attracts teachers for several reasons: stable income, free housing, comprehensive benefits, and immersion in Korean culture. Many teachers stay for multiple years. Some use it as a launchpad for careers in education, translation, or other work in Asia.

## What You'll Do

### Daily Reality

You work 8 hours per day, typically 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM. Of those hours, you teach approximately 22 class periods per week—the rest is preparation time, grading, and assisting your co-teacher.

Class lengths vary by school level:
- **Elementary school:** 40-minute periods
- **Middle school:** 45-minute periods
- **High school:** 50-minute periods

You always work with a Korean co-teacher who is supposed to share classroom management and lesson planning. In practice, the quality of this partnership varies enormously. Some co-teachers are fluent in English and highly collaborative. Others speak limited English and leave you to run the class alone.

### Class Sizes and Students

Class sizes typically range from 20 to 40 students. Some schools cap classes at 35, but rural schools may have as few as 10 students.

Student motivation varies. Unlike hagwon students whose parents pay for extra English instruction, public school students take English as a required subject. Some are enthusiastic. Many are not.

You follow the national curriculum but often have flexibility in how you teach it. Many teachers develop games, activities, and cultural lessons to make classes engaging.

### School Placement

You have limited control over where you are placed. You can request metropolitan areas (Seoul, Busan, Incheon, Daegu) or rural regions, but EPIK assigns your final placement based on school needs.

**You typically do not know your placement until after signing your contract.** You might end up in central Seoul with one school. You might end up in a rural province, traveling between four different schools by bus.

Rural placements often come with isolation but also closer community bonds, lower cost of living, and sometimes bonus pay.

## Requirements

### Non-Negotiable Requirements

- **Bachelor's degree:** From an accredited university in your home country. Any major is acceptable. This is a legal requirement for the E-2 teaching visa—Korea will not issue a work visa without it.

- **Citizenship:** You must be a citizen of one of seven countries: USA, UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, or South Africa. Indian citizens are also eligible under the CEPA agreement if they hold a valid teaching certificate in English education—a TEFL/TESOL/CELTA does not satisfy this requirement.

- **Age:** Minimum 20 years old (age 18+)

- **Clean criminal record:** FBI background check (for Americans) or equivalent national-level check, apostilled

- **Health requirements:** Pass a health examination after arrival in Korea

### TEFL Certification

TEFL requirements depend on your degree:

- **Education-related degree** (B.Ed., M.Ed., TESOL, Teaching, Second Language Studies): No TEFL certificate required

- **Any other degree:** Minimum 100-hour TEFL/TESOL/CELTA certificate from an accredited program

**Special requirements by region:** Some regions require in-person/practicum hours as part of your TEFL certification:
- **Busan:** At least 50 hours must be in-person classroom instruction
- **Seoul and Gyeongnam:** At least 20 hours of in-class/practicum component required
- **All other regions:** No in-person requirement; fully online certificates accepted

EPIK will only count in-class hours that are part of your TEFL curriculum. Volunteering extra hours outside the program does not count.

The TEFL certificate can be completed during the application process—you do not need it when you first apply, but you must have it six weeks before your arrival date.

### Physical Requirements

Teaching requires standing for extended periods. You should be comfortable on your feet for 4-6 hours daily.

### Time Commitment

- **Contract length:** 12 months
- **Renewal option:** Yes, with salary increases for each additional year
- **Work hours:** 40 hours per week (22 teaching hours)
- **Vacation:** 18+ paid vacation days plus all Korean national holidays

## Training Provided

### EPIK Orientation

All new teachers attend a week-long orientation upon arrival in Korea. This includes:

- Korean language basics
- Cultural adjustment training
- Classroom management techniques
- Lesson planning workshops
- Introduction to Korean school culture

The orientation is mandatory and paid. However, former teachers report that orientation is more applicable to urban placements than rural ones. If you end up in a small town, you may need to figure out local logistics on your own.

### Ongoing Development

Your school may offer additional professional development, but this varies widely. Many teachers develop skills through experience and peer networks rather than formal training.

### What You Learn

After a year in EPIK, you will have:
- Classroom management experience with large groups
- Lesson planning and curriculum adaptation skills
- Cross-cultural communication abilities
- Basic Korean language skills (if you study)
- Understanding of Korean workplace culture

These skills transfer to other teaching positions, international education roles, and any job requiring cross-cultural competence.

## The Money Reality

### Monthly Salary (2025-2026)

Salaries are based on qualifications and range from Level 1 (highest) to Level 3 (lowest):

| Level | Monthly Pay (KRW) | Approximate USD |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Level 1 | 2,700,000 - 2,800,000 | $2,000 - $2,100 |
| Level 2 | 2,300,000 - 2,500,000 | $1,700 - $1,900 |
| Level 3 | 2,000,000 - 2,100,000 | $1,500 - $1,600 |

**Level determination:**
- Level 1: Master's degree OR Bachelor's + teaching license + experience
- Level 2: Bachelor's degree + TEFL + some teaching experience
- Level 3: Bachelor's degree + TEFL, no experience

Most first-time teachers start at Level 2. **Important: EPIK Offices of Education are currently not hiring Level 3 applicants.** You must qualify for at least Level 2 before signing your final contract.

Salaries increase with each contract renewal, and some rural areas offer location bonuses.

### Housing

**Provided free.** Your school arranges and pays for a furnished apartment near your workplace.

Standard apartment furnishings include:
- Bed
- Refrigerator
- Washing machine
- Stove/cooktop
- Air conditioning unit

**You pay for utilities:** electricity, water, gas, and internet. Budget 100,000-200,000 KRW monthly ($75-$150) for utilities.

In rare cases where the school cannot arrange housing, you receive a monthly housing allowance of 400,000-500,000 KRW ($300-$375).

### Startup Costs

Before you earn anything, you will spend:

| Item | Cost (USD) | Notes |
|------|------------|-------|
| TEFL certification | $200 - $1,500 | Depends on program; 100+ hour online courses are cheapest |
| FBI background check | $18 + channeler fees | FBI directly takes 12+ weeks; channelers cost $50-150 but are faster |
| Apostille fees | $20 - $100 | Varies by state/country |
| Diploma apostille | $20 - $100 | Must be notarized first |
| Health check | $100 - $300 | Done in Korea, paid upfront |
| Initial living expenses | $500 - $1,000 | First month before payday |
| One-way flight | $800 - $1,500 | Reimbursed, but paid upfront |

**Total startup costs: $1,500 - $4,000+**

You will spend money before you earn money. Plan accordingly.

### Reimbursements and Bonuses

**Entrance Allowance:** 1,800,000 KRW ($1,350) paid at the end of your first month. Some regions (Busan, Daegu, Jeju, Incheon, Chungbuk) delay this until the 6-month mark. **Important:** This allowance is technically issued as a loan. If you terminate your contract within the first six months for any reason, you must repay the full amount immediately.

**Settlement Allowance:** 300,000 KRW ($225) within your first month—first contract only.

**Flight Reimbursement:** Inbound flight is reimbursed. Return flight provided or reimbursed upon contract completion.

**Contract Completion Bonus:** 1,300,000 KRW ($975) at the end of each contract. **Not provided in Seoul, Jeju, Gyeonggi, or Daejeon** until you complete your final contract.

**Exit Allowance:** 1,300,000 KRW ($975) at the end of your final contract—meant to cover return travel.

**Severance Pay:** Approximately one month's salary, paid only when you complete all contracts and leave the employer (not paid between renewals).

**Pension Refund:** Americans, Canadians, and Australians can claim a lump-sum refund of pension contributions when leaving Korea. Other nationalities cannot.

### Health Insurance

National health insurance is provided. You pay 50%, your employer pays 50%. Deducted from your paycheck—usually around 100,000 KRW monthly ($75).

### Realistic Savings

With free housing and modest living, most EPIK teachers save $500-$1,000 per month. If you live frugally in a rural area, you can save more. If you travel every weekend and eat out constantly, you will save less.

## How to Get Started

The entire process takes 4-6 months. Start early.

### Step 1: Confirm Eligibility

1. Verify you have (or will have) a bachelor's degree from an accredited university
2. Confirm you are a citizen of USA, UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, or South Africa
3. Ensure you have no criminal record

### Step 2: Timeline Your Application

EPIK hires for two intakes per year:

- **Spring intake:** Applications open August 1st, placement in late February-April
- **Fall intake:** Applications open February 1st, placement in late August-October

Apply 4-6 months before your desired start date.

### Step 3: Begin Background Check Immediately

For U.S. citizens, the FBI background check takes 12+ weeks through official channels. Options:

- **FBI Identity History Summary:** $18, takes 12+ weeks
- **FBI-Approved Channelers:** $50-150, takes 2-4 weeks

Request your check as soon as you decide to apply. It expires after 6 months, so time it carefully.

### Step 4: Get Your Apostilles

Both your criminal background check and diploma require apostilles (international authentication). In the U.S., this goes through your state's Secretary of State office.

The diploma must first be notarized, then apostilled. Start this process early.

### Step 5: Submit Online Application

Go to [epik.go.kr](https://www.epik.go.kr) and complete the application, which includes:

- Personal information
- Education history
- Essay responses
- Placement preferences

### Step 6: Interview

If your application is accepted, you will have a video interview with an EPIK representative. This assesses your communication skills, motivation, and cultural adaptability.

### Step 7: Complete TEFL (If Needed)

If you don't have an education degree, complete your 100+ hour TEFL certification. This must be done at least 6 weeks before arrival.

Recommended affordable options:
- International TEFL Academy (in-person and online)
- CIEE TEFL (online)
- University-based programs

### Step 8: Submit Final Documents

After acceptance, submit hard copies of:
- Apostilled criminal background check (original)
- Apostilled diploma copy (notarized first)
- Health statement
- Contract documents

### Step 9: Receive Placement and Visa

EPIK issues your placement and provides documents for your E-2 visa. Apply at the Korean consulate in your country.

### Step 10: Attend Orientation

Fly to Korea, attend the mandatory week-long orientation, then travel to your school placement.

## The Hard Parts

### Culture Shock is Real

You will be confused, frustrated, and lonely at times. Korean workplace culture differs significantly from Western expectations:

- Hierarchy matters. Age and position determine how you interact with colleagues.
- Indirect communication is the norm. "That might be difficult" often means "no."
- After-work socializing (and drinking) is expected. Refusing repeatedly may affect relationships.
- Lunch is communal. Eating alone is unusual.

Many teachers report the first 3 months being the hardest. The excitement wears off, isolation sets in, and small frustrations compound.

### Workplace Isolation

You may be the only non-Korean at your school. All your coworkers speak Korean to each other. Staff meetings happen in Korean. You sit at a desk in a room full of people having conversations you cannot understand.

This is draining. Even with a supportive co-teacher, you may feel like an outsider for months.

### Co-Teacher Lottery

Your experience depends heavily on your co-teacher, and you have no control over who that is. Possibilities include:

- **Best case:** A collaborative partner who speaks English well, shares lesson planning, and helps you navigate school culture
- **Worst case:** Someone who speaks minimal English, leaves you to manage 35 students alone, and resents your presence

Most co-teachers fall somewhere in between. Adapting to this relationship is a major challenge.

### Rural Placement Realities

If placed in a rural area:

- You may have no other foreigners in your town
- Public transportation may be limited or nonexistent
- English signage and English-speaking services will be rare
- You may need to travel hours to reach a major city

This can be isolating, especially in winter when days are short and cold.

### Language Barrier

Koreans do not generally speak English in daily life. Ordering food, going to the doctor, dealing with your landlord—all of this requires Korean language skills or creative miming.

EPIK orientation provides basic Korean, but becoming functional takes months of dedicated study.

### You Don't Choose Your School

You request a region, but EPIK places you where schools need teachers. You might get exactly what you wanted. You might get the opposite.

Once placed, switching schools is difficult. You are generally expected to complete your contract at your assigned location.

## Where This Can Lead

### Continue Teaching in Korea

Many EPIK teachers renew for multiple years. Benefits:
- Guaranteed salary increases each year
- Deeper cultural integration
- Stronger Korean language skills
- Established social network

Some teachers stay 3-5 years. A few make it a decade-long commitment.

### Private Academy (Hagwon) Teaching

After EPIK, many teachers move to hagwons for:
- Higher base salaries (often $2,200-$2,800/month)
- Urban locations (hagwons cluster in cities)
- Different student populations (motivated, paying students)

Trade-offs: Less job security, longer hours (often 2-9 PM), smaller vacation allotments, and variable employer quality.

### Teaching Elsewhere in Asia

EPIK experience qualifies you for teaching positions across Asia:

- **Japan (JET Program):** Government program similar to EPIK, also requires bachelor's degree
- **Taiwan (FET Program):** Government public school program
- **China:** Large private school market, varying quality
- **Vietnam and Thailand:** Lower pay but lower cost of living; some positions do not require degrees

Korea is often considered the best first step due to high pay, good benefits, and strong legal protections.

### International School Teaching

With EPIK experience plus additional credentials (teaching license, M.Ed.), you can pursue international school positions that pay $3,000-$6,000+ monthly with housing.

### Non-Teaching Paths

Skills developed in EPIK transfer to:
- **Translation and interpretation** (if you learn Korean)
- **Curriculum development** for ESL publishers
- **Corporate training** in English communication
- **Study abroad advising**
- **Immigration and visa consulting**

### Graduate School

Many teachers use EPIK savings to fund graduate degrees. One year of living expenses saved in Korea can cover a significant portion of master's program costs.

## EPIK vs. Hagwons

If you are considering teaching in Korea, you will choose between EPIK (public schools) and hagwons (private academies). Here is an honest comparison:

| Factor | EPIK | Hagwon |
|--------|------|--------|
| **Salary** | 2.0-2.8M KRW/month | 2.2-3.0M KRW/month (often higher) |
| **Hours** | 8:30 AM - 4:30 PM | Usually 2:00 PM - 9:00 PM; some split schedules |
| **Teaching load** | 22 hours/week | 25-30 hours/week |
| **Vacation** | 18+ days + national holidays | 10-15 days; employer controls scheduling |
| **Sick days** | 11 days | Usually 3 days |
| **Housing** | Always provided | Usually provided; quality varies |
| **Job security** | Government contract; very stable | Private business; schools can close |
| **Location control** | Limited (assigned by EPIK) | You negotiate directly |
| **Hiring timeline** | Twice yearly (Spring, Fall) | Year-round |
| **Application process** | 4-6 months, competitive | 1-2 months, many positions |
| **Students** | Required subject; mixed motivation | Paying customers; often motivated |
| **Co-teacher** | Always present (quality varies) | Usually teach alone |

**Choose EPIK if:** You want stability, normal working hours, more vacation, and government protections.

**Choose a hagwon if:** You want to choose your city, start sooner, potentially earn more, and prefer smaller class sizes.

Hagwons have a reputation for problematic employers—withheld pay, broken contracts, sudden closures. Many are legitimate, but research individual schools carefully. EPIK eliminates this risk through government oversight.
