# All Hands and Hearts

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** Claude (Opus 4.5)

## Overview

All Hands and Hearts (formerly All Hands Volunteers) is a disaster relief nonprofit that deploys volunteers for rebuilding and recovery work worldwide. Unlike many "voluntourism" programs, **there is no program fee**—housing and meals are provided free.

For Sam, this offers hands-on construction experience, international travel opportunities, and meaningful service work with minimal upfront cost. The only expenses are travel to the site and personal gear.

## What You'll Do

Work varies by disaster phase:

**Phase 1: Emergency Response**
- Mucking out flooded homes
- Gutting water-damaged structures
- Debris removal
- Chainsaw work
- Mold sanitation

**Phase 2: Recovery**
- Home repairs
- Roofing
- Framing
- Drywall installation
- Painting

**Phase 3: Rebuild**
- School reconstruction
- Water and sanitation systems
- Community infrastructure

**Typical Schedule:** Monday-Friday (some programs Monday-Saturday), 8am-4pm during deployment.

## Requirements

- **Age:** 18+ for residential volunteers (16-17 can participate as day volunteers with a parent)
- **Background Check:** Required (typically $25-50 in the US)
- **Skills:** None required—all training provided on-site
- **Physical:** Must be able to perform manual labor (lifting, carrying, working on feet)
- **Mental:** Prepared for emotionally demanding environment (disaster survivors, destroyed communities)
- **Time:** No strict minimum (varies by program); maximum initial booking is 2 months

## Training Provided

All training happens on-site at no cost:
- Construction fundamentals (framing, drywall, roofing basics)
- Power tool operation
- Safety protocols
- Mold remediation procedures

No formal certifications result, but you gain practical construction skills transferable to trade careers.

## The Money Reality

### What's Provided (Free)
- Housing (communal accommodations—churches, schools, or volunteer bases)
- Three meals per day on working days
- Travel insurance for international volunteers under age 80
- Personal protective equipment (PPE)
- Volunteer shirt

### What You Pay
| Expense | Estimated Cost |
|---------|---------------|
| Travel to/from program site | $200-$1,500+ (depending on location) |
| Background check | $25-50 (US) |
| Work boots and clothes | $100-200 |
| Meals on days off | $50-100/week |
| Vaccinations (international) | $0-300 |
| **Total startup** | **$375-2,150** |

### No Income

This is volunteer work. You earn nothing. Plan finances accordingly—you'll need savings or another income source before and after.

## How to Get Started

1. **Check current programs at allhandsandhearts.org/volunteer** — Programs change based on active disasters

2. **Complete online application** — Basic info and availability

3. **Get background check** — Instructions provided after application

4. **Book travel** — You arrange and pay for your own transportation

5. **Arrive at program site** — Orientation happens on arrival

## The Hard Parts

**Physically exhausting.** Construction labor in disaster conditions—debris, mud, heat, or cold. Long days on your feet.

**Basic living conditions.** Communal sleeping (mixed-gender dorms or shared rooms), limited privacy, basic facilities. Not a resort.

**Emotionally heavy.** You're in disaster zones working with people who've lost everything. Some volunteers experience secondary traumatic stress.

**No pay.** You're spending money (on travel) and time without income. Requires financial cushion.

**Unpredictable conditions.** Disaster sites have ongoing challenges—weather, logistics, changing needs.

**Past safety concerns.** The organization faced serious allegations of mishandled harassment and assault cases in 2021, leading to leadership changes and organizational reforms. The founder/chairman resigned in December 2021. Since then, the organization has implemented new safeguarding policies and published accountability commitments. Research their current safety policies before committing.
