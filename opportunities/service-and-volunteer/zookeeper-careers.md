# Zookeeper / Zoo Careers

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** Fact Checker Agent

## Overview

Zookeeping is passion work. You care for animals in accredited zoos and aquariums—feeding, cleaning, monitoring health, providing enrichment, and educating the public. The work is physically demanding, emotionally rewarding, and poorly compensated.

This opportunity might work for young adults because many entry-level positions do not require a four-year degree. Experience often matters more than education. You can start through volunteer programs, internships, or entry-level support roles and work your way up. The path is slow but accessible.

The catch: competition is intense. Thousands of people want these jobs. The pay is low—most keepers earn $28,000-$45,000 annually. You will clean animal waste for 60% of your workday. You work holidays, weekends, and early mornings. You must relocate to wherever the jobs exist. And advancing from entry-level to senior keeper can take years of low-paid labor.

If you love animals more than you love comfortable paychecks, this path exists. But go in with realistic expectations.

## What You'll Do

The marketing version of zookeeping shows people cuddling baby animals and training dolphins. The reality is different.

**Daily Tasks (What 80% of the Job Actually Looks Like)**

- Clean enclosures and remove animal waste
- Prepare diets—chopping vegetables, weighing portions, mixing supplements
- Feed animals on schedule, multiple times daily
- Scrub water features and maintain exhibit cleanliness
- Observe animals for signs of illness, injury, or behavioral changes
- Document everything—food intake, behavior, health observations
- Maintain equipment and safety barriers

**The Remaining 20%**

- Design and implement enrichment activities—puzzle feeders, novel objects, training sessions
- Conduct training sessions using operant conditioning
- Assist veterinary staff during exams and procedures
- Give educational talks to visitors
- Participate in breeding programs and species survival plans
- Collaborate with researchers on behavioral studies

A typical day starts at 6:00 or 7:00 AM. You check on animals, assess their condition, and begin feeding rounds. Then you clean—and keep cleaning. Exhibit maintenance is constant. You prepare diets for the next day. You document observations. You might squeeze in training sessions between cleaning duties.

The work happens in all weather. Hot summers. Cold winters. Rain. Snow. Your animals need care regardless of conditions outside.

One keeper described the reality: "About 60% of my job is cleaning, which is something most people don't expect."

## Requirements

**Age and Eligibility**

- Most positions require you to be 18+
- Some teen volunteer programs accept 14-17 year olds
- No citizenship requirements for most positions—varies by employer

**Education**

This is where it gets complicated. Officially, many job postings list a bachelor's degree as required or preferred. In practice, experience can substitute for formal education.

- **High school diploma:** Sufficient for entry-level support roles—grounds crew, guest services, food service
- **Associate degree:** Opens doors at teaching zoos and some smaller facilities. Programs at Santa Fe College, Moorpark College, and Pikes Peak Community College specifically train zookeepers
- **Bachelor's degree:** Preferred by most AZA-accredited facilities. Common majors include biology, zoology, animal science, wildlife management, or psychology
- **Experience in lieu of degree:** Some facilities accept equivalent animal care experience—vet clinics, farms, animal shelters, wildlife rehabilitation centers

The honest truth: almost every zookeeper today holds at minimum an associate degree, with an overwhelming majority holding bachelor's degrees. Getting hired without a degree is possible but increasingly difficult.

**Experience**

This is the more important requirement. Expect to accumulate 1,000-2,000+ hours of animal-related experience before landing your first keeper position.

Sources of acceptable experience:
- Zoo volunteer programs (30-200 hours minimum before keeper aide eligibility)
- Zoo internships (often unpaid, lasting 3-6 months)
- Veterinary clinic or hospital work
- Animal shelter or rescue work
- Farm experience
- Wildlife rehabilitation centers
- Pet care facilities

**Physical Requirements**

This job is hard on your body:
- Lift and carry 50+ pounds regularly (feed bags, equipment)
- Stand and walk for 8+ hours daily
- Work outdoors in all weather conditions
- Bend, stoop, and work in cramped spaces
- Risk of bites, scratches, kicks, and other animal-related injuries
- 55% of zoo veterinarians report experiencing back strain during their careers—keepers face similar physical stress

**Time Commitment**

- Full-time positions: 40 hours per week
- Early morning starts (6:00-7:00 AM typical)
- Weekend and holiday work required—animals need care every day
- Seasonal positions available at some facilities

## Training Provided

Most zoos provide on-the-job training covering:
- Facility protocols and safety procedures
- Species-specific husbandry for your assigned area
- Diet preparation and feeding methods
- Enrichment design and implementation
- Basic operant conditioning and training techniques
- Record-keeping systems
- Visitor interaction and public speaking

**Certifications You Can Earn**

- First aid and CPR
- SCUBA certification (for aquarium work—required for aquarist/diver positions)
- Specialized animal handling certificates

**Professional Development**

The American Association of Zoo Keepers (AAZK) offers professional development workshops, certificates, and networking opportunities. Annual conference attendance builds skills and connections.

## The Money Reality

### Pay

Zookeepers are underpaid relative to their education level. This is the central truth of the profession.

| Position | Hourly Range | Annual Range | Notes |
|----------|-------------|--------------|-------|
| Keeper Aide / Assistant | $11-15/hr | $23,000-31,000 | Entry-level, often part-time |
| Animal Keeper I | $14-18/hr | $29,000-37,000 | First full keeper role |
| Animal Keeper II | $16-21/hr | $33,000-44,000 | 2-4 years experience |
| Senior Keeper | $19-25/hr | $40,000-52,000 | 5+ years, team lead |
| Area Supervisor | $22-30/hr | $46,000-62,000 | Management responsibilities |
| Curator | $50,000-80,000+ | | Advanced degree often required |

According to BLS data (May 2024), the median annual wage for animal caretakers—which includes zookeepers—was $33,470. The lowest 10% earned less than $24,500. The highest 10% earned more than $46,480.

Pay varies significantly by:
- **Facility type:** City/state-run union zoos pay better ($20-25/hr). Small private zoos may pay $10-12/hr.
- **Location:** Cost-of-living adjustments vary. California zoos pay more than rural midwest facilities.
- **Specialization:** Marine mammal trainers and positions requiring SCUBA certification sometimes pay premiums.

### Housing

Zoos rarely provide housing. You are responsible for finding and paying for your own accommodations near the facility.

**Exception:** Some seasonal positions or internships at remote facilities may include basic housing or housing assistance.

### Startup Costs

| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Education | $0 - $40,000+ | Associate degree: $5,000-15,000. Bachelor's: $20,000-40,000+ |
| Volunteer hours | Time investment | 100-500 hours unpaid work to become competitive |
| Internship | Time + living costs | Often unpaid, lasting 3-6 months |
| Relocation | $1,000-5,000 | Moving to where jobs exist |
| Work gear | $200-500 | Boots, appropriate clothing |
| SCUBA certification | $300-800 | If pursuing aquarium/marine work |

**The hidden cost:** Years of low-paid or unpaid labor building experience before landing a living-wage position.

### Benefits

Full-time employees at AZA-accredited facilities typically receive:
- Health insurance
- Dental and vision coverage
- Retirement plan contributions
- Paid time off
- Free or discounted zoo admission

Part-time, seasonal, and intern positions rarely include benefits.

### Hidden Costs

- **Geographic mobility:** You go where the jobs are. This may mean leaving family and friends.
- **Physical wear:** Back problems, injuries, and repetitive strain are common long-term.
- **Emotional labor:** Animals die. Euthanasia decisions happen. This takes a toll.
- **Career ceiling:** Most keeper positions top out around $50,000. Higher earnings require moving into management—which means less animal work.

## How to Get Started

These are concrete steps you can take this week:

1. **Find local zoos and aquariums.** Make a list of all AZA-accredited facilities within reasonable distance. Check the AZA directory at [aza.org](https://www.aza.org/find-a-zoo-or-aquarium).

2. **Apply to volunteer programs.** Most zoos have formal volunteer applications. Expect to commit 4-8 hours weekly for at least 6 months. Common requirements:
   - Phoenix Zoo: 200 hours before eligible for animal-adjacent volunteer roles
   - Houston Zoo: 30 hours before keeper aide application eligibility
   - Many zoos: 60-80 hours annually minimum

3. **Look for entry-level support positions.** These get your foot in the door:
   - Guest services / admissions
   - Food service
   - Retail / gift shop
   - Grounds crew / horticulture
   - Janitorial / facilities

4. **Search current openings.** Use these resources:
   - [AZA Job Board](https://www.aza.org/jobs)
   - [ZoosHiring.com](https://zooshiring.com/)
   - Individual zoo career pages
   - Indeed and LinkedIn (search "animal keeper" or "zookeeper")

5. **Consider teaching zoo programs.** If you want focused training without a four-year degree:
   - Santa Fe College Teaching Zoo (Florida) — Associate degree, AZA-accredited facility on campus, 1,800+ hours hands-on experience
   - Moorpark College EATM Program (California) — Associate degree, America's Teaching Zoo, highly competitive
   - Pikes Peak Community College (Colorado) — Zoo Keeper / Zoo Technology program

6. **Build animal experience anywhere.** Apply to:
   - Local animal shelters
   - Veterinary clinics (even receptionist roles help)
   - Pet care facilities
   - Farms (including urban farms)
   - Wildlife rehabilitation centers

7. **Join AAZK.** Membership starts at $45/year for students. Benefits include job postings, professional development, and networking.

## The Hard Parts

**Competition is fierce.** A single keeper position can receive 50-200+ applications. Entry-level openings at major zoos are especially competitive. Many applicants have degrees and extensive experience.

**The pay never really improves.** Unlike fields where experience leads to significantly higher earnings, keeper salaries plateau. After 10 years, you might earn $45,000-50,000. The only path to higher income is management—which means less direct animal work.

**You will clean constantly.** Removing animal waste is the majority of the job. If you find this degrading or tedious, zookeeping is not for you.

**The work is physically punishing.** Back injuries are common. You lift heavy objects daily. You work in extreme temperatures. Your body will feel the job.

**Relocation is usually required.** Unless you live near an AZA zoo, you will move. Career advancement often requires moving again. Building stable community ties is difficult.

**Emotional demands are real.** Animals you care for will die. Some deaths will be unexpected. Some will involve euthanasia decisions. Burnout happens.

**Career entry takes years.** The path from interested outsider to paid keeper typically takes 2-5 years of volunteer work, education, and internships—much of it unpaid or low-paid.

**Holiday and weekend work is mandatory.** Animals need care 365 days a year. Expect to work Christmas, Thanksgiving, and summer weekends.

## AZA vs. Roadside Zoos

Not all zoos are created equal. This matters for ethics and for your career.

**AZA-Accredited Facilities**

The Association of Zoos and Aquariums accredits fewer than 250 facilities in the U.S.—less than 10% of licensed animal exhibitors. These facilities:
- Meet rigorous animal welfare standards reviewed every 5 years
- Participate in Species Survival Plans for endangered animals
- Fund conservation and research programs
- Provide professional development for staff
- Offer transferable experience recognized industry-wide

**Roadside Zoos and Unaccredited Facilities**

Many smaller operations exist outside accreditation:
- Standards vary wildly—some are ethical, many are not
- May engage in practices AZA prohibits (cub petting, elephant rides)
- Experience may not transfer to AZA facilities
- Working conditions and pay are often worse
- Some are motivated primarily by profit rather than conservation

**Recommendation:** Start your career at an AZA-accredited facility if possible. Experience there transfers anywhere. Experience at unaccredited facilities may not.

Check accreditation status at [aza.org/find-a-zoo-or-aquarium](https://www.aza.org/find-a-zoo-or-aquarium).

## Specializations

After gaining general keeper experience, many keepers specialize:

| Specialization | Notes |
|----------------|-------|
| **Primates** | Highly competitive, requires extensive training experience |
| **Big Cats** | Dangerous animal protocols, additional safety training |
| **Elephants** | Protected contact training, physical demands |
| **Marine Mammals** | SCUBA certification often required, separate career track |
| **Birds / Avian** | Flight and behavior management, often separate from mammal areas |
| **Reptiles / Herpetology** | Venomous animal protocols for some |
| **Aquarium / Fish** | Water chemistry knowledge, diving for larger exhibits |
| **Education Animals** | Animal ambassadors, training-focused, public interaction |

Specialization happens after you prove yourself as a general keeper. Expect 2-4 years before you can request specific animal assignments.

## Career Progression

The typical path:

1. **Volunteer** (0-2 years) — Unpaid, building hours and connections
2. **Intern** (3-12 months) — Often unpaid, intensive experience
3. **Keeper Aide / Part-Time Keeper** — Entry paid position, $11-15/hr
4. **Animal Keeper I** — First full-time keeper role, $14-18/hr
5. **Animal Keeper II / III** — Increasing responsibility, $16-22/hr
6. **Senior Keeper** — Team lead, mentoring, $19-25/hr
7. **Area Supervisor** — Managing multiple keeper teams
8. **Assistant Curator / Curator** — Department leadership, advanced degree often required
9. **General Curator** — Senior management
10. **Zoo Director** — Rare endpoint, executive responsibilities

Most keepers remain at the keeper level. Advancement to curator requires additional education (often a master's degree) and management experience. Director positions are extremely rare.

**Timeline:** Reaching senior keeper typically takes 5-10 years. Curator positions require 10-15+ years plus advanced degrees.

## Disney's Animal Kingdom

Disney's Animal Kingdom in Orlando is one of the largest zoo employers. Requirements are similar to other AZA facilities but often more competitive.

**Animal Keeper Requirements:**
- One year paid animal husbandry experience at an AZA-accredited facility, OR
- Two years of verifiable college coursework (60 semester hours)
- Experience with operant conditioning preferred
- Experience with African species (ungulates, carnivores, equids) preferred

**Disney Internships:**
- Animal Husbandry Internship
- Veterinary Hospital Internship
- Animal Nutrition Center Internship
- Conservation Education Internship

All require prior animal experience. These are highly competitive—hundreds apply for a handful of spots.

**Benefits:** Disney offers competitive pay for the industry, comprehensive benefits, and strong advancement opportunities within the Disney parks system.

## Where This Can Lead

Skills developed as a zookeeper transfer to:
- **Wildlife rehabilitation centers** — Similar animal care skills
- **Wildlife education / outreach** — Public speaking, curriculum development
- **Veterinary technician** — With additional certification
- **Conservation fieldwork** — Research and monitoring
- **Animal sanctuary work** — Often similar pay but different mission
- **Government wildlife agencies** — Fish & Wildlife Service, state agencies
- **Zoo education department** — Less animal care, more teaching
- **Zoo management** — With additional education

The broader value: you develop practical animal husbandry skills, public communication abilities, teamwork experience, and documented passion that employers in related fields recognize.
