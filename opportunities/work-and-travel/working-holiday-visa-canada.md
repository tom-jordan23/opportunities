# Working Holiday Visa - Canada (IEC)

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-08
> **Fact Checker:** Fact Checker Agent

## Overview

Canada's International Experience Canada (IEC) program lets young people work and travel in Canada for up to 12 months. The catch for US citizens: the United States does not have a direct Youth Mobility Agreement with Canada. You cannot apply directly through the government portal. Instead, you must go through a Recognized Organization (RO) that sponsors your application.

This adds cost and complexity. But if you want legal authorization to work any job in Canada for a year—ski resorts in Whistler, restaurants in Toronto, farms in the Okanagan—the IEC Working Holiday permit is the way to do it.

The program operates on a lottery system. Getting selected is not guaranteed. This is the single biggest hurdle for Americans trying to use this path.

## What You'll Do

A Working Holiday permit is an open work permit. You can work for any employer in Canada, doing any job. You are not tied to a specific employer or location.

Common jobs for Working Holiday participants:

**Ski resorts (winter):**
- Lift operators, rental attendants, ski instructors
- Guest services, food and beverage
- Housekeeping, snowmaking
- Pay: CAD $16-23 per hour plus tips where applicable
- Many positions include subsidized staff housing (CAD $150-200 per week)

**Summer resorts and tourism:**
- Hospitality roles in Banff, Jasper, and mountain towns
- Tour guiding, outdoor activity operators
- Restaurant and bar work
- Similar pay range with potential for staff housing

**Urban jobs (Vancouver, Toronto):**
- Restaurant, retail, hospitality
- Higher wages but much higher living costs
- No staff housing—you find your own apartment

**Farm work:**
- Seasonal harvest work across British Columbia, Ontario, Quebec
- Some farms provide accommodation

The reality is that most Working Holiday participants take entry-level hospitality and service jobs. The value is not the job itself. The value is the year of legal work authorization to explore Canada while earning money.

## Requirements

### Age and Eligibility

- **Age:** 18 to 35 years old at time of application
- **Citizenship:** US citizens qualify through Recognized Organizations
- **Passport:** Valid US passport that covers your intended stay
- **Criminal record:** Must have no criminal record (you self-declare; serious offenses result in denial)
- **Health:** Must be in good health
- **Dependents:** Cannot be accompanied by dependents

### Going Through a Recognized Organization

US citizens cannot apply directly to the IEC pool. You must apply through a Recognized Organization that has a quota of spots for non-IEC countries.

**Recognized Organizations for US citizens:**

| Organization | Program Fee | Notes |
|--------------|-------------|-------|
| SWAP Working Holidays | ~CAD $2,100 | Partners with InterExchange for US applicants |
| InterExchange | ~USD $1,795 | Main US-based option |

These fees are in addition to the government visa fees. The organizations provide:
- Nomination for IEC (required for US citizens)
- Application guidance
- Pre-departure orientations
- Job search resources and databases
- In-country support in Toronto and Vancouver
- Community access and social events

### Documents Required

- Valid US passport
- Police certificate if requested
- Proof of funds (bank statement)
- Proof of health insurance
- Resume (for job searching, not required for visa)

## The Money Reality

### Total Upfront Costs for US Citizens

| Expense | Approximate Cost |
|---------|------------------|
| Recognized Organization fee | USD $1,795 - CAD $2,100 |
| IEC participation fee | CAD $184.75 |
| Open work permit fee | CAD $100 |
| Biometrics fee | CAD $85 (US citizens may be exempt) |
| Health insurance (12 months) | USD $600-1,800 |
| Flight to Canada | USD $300-600 |
| Proof of funds requirement | CAD $2,500 minimum |
| **Total before arriving** | **USD $3,500-5,500+** |

This is a significant upfront investment. You need roughly USD $3,000-4,000 in fees and insurance, plus CAD $2,500 in your bank account to show at the border, plus your flight. Budget at least USD $5,000-6,000 total before you earn your first Canadian paycheck.

### What You Can Earn

**Provincial minimum wages (2026):**
- British Columbia: CAD $17.85/hour
- Alberta: CAD $15.00/hour
- Ontario: CAD $17.60/hour
- Quebec: CAD $16.10/hour

**Realistic earnings:**
- Entry-level hospitality: CAD $16-21/hour
- With tips (restaurant, bar): CAD $20-30+/hour effective
- Ski resort with housing: CAD $2,400-3,200/month before deductions

**Cost of living reality:**
- Vancouver one-bedroom apartment: ~CAD $2,600/month
- Staff housing at resorts: CAD $400-800/month
- Food if not included: CAD $400-600/month

**The math for saving money:**

If you work at a ski resort with subsidized housing and meals:
- Gross pay: CAD $2,800/month (35 hrs/week at $20/hr)
- Housing: -CAD $600
- Food, phone, incidentals: -CAD $400
- Remaining: CAD $1,800/month

Over a winter season (5 months): CAD $9,000 saved.

If you live in Vancouver without staff housing:
- Gross pay: CAD $3,000/month
- Rent: -CAD $2,600 (even with roommates, expect $1,300+)
- Food, transport, phone: -CAD $600
- Remaining: Near zero or negative

**Bottom line:** Jobs with staff housing make financial sense. Urban work in expensive cities often results in breaking even or losing money after your upfront investment.

### Tax Implications

- Canada will withhold income tax from your paychecks
- As a non-resident, you may qualify for a tax refund when you leave
- You must still file US taxes on worldwide income as a US citizen
- Keep all pay stubs and tax documents

## How to Get Started

### Step 1: Check Your Timeline

The IEC season runs annually. Pools typically open in December for the following year. Applications are processed through the year until quotas fill.

**2026 season:** Pools opened December 19, 2025. Invitations began rolling out January 2026.

Recognized Organizations have their own deadlines and waitlists. Spots are limited. For the 2026 season, SWAP's waitlist for US citizens closed in late 2025.

### Step 2: Research Recognized Organizations

Visit these organizations' websites:
1. **InterExchange:** interexchange.org/programs/travel-experiences-for-u-s-residents/work-travel-canada/
2. **SWAP Working Holidays:** swap.ca

Compare their fees, services, and current availability. Contact them directly to confirm US citizen eligibility and 2026/2027 timelines.

### Step 3: Apply to a Recognized Organization

Once you choose an organization:
1. Complete their application and pay their program fee
2. Provide required documents (passport, proof of funds, etc.)
3. They will nominate you for an IEC spot

Unlike direct IEC applicants who enter a lottery pool, Recognized Organizations have reserved spots. Getting accepted by the RO typically means you will get an IEC invitation—but spaces are limited.

### Step 4: Complete Your IEC Application

After receiving an Invitation to Apply (ITA) through your RO:
1. Accept the invitation within 10 days
2. Submit your work permit application within 20 days
3. Pay government fees (CAD $284.75 for participation + work permit; add CAD $85 for biometrics if required)
4. Submit biometrics at a designated collection point within 30 days of receiving your Biometric Instruction Letter
5. Wait for processing (typically 5-8 weeks)

### Step 5: Receive Your Port of Entry Letter

If approved, you receive a Port of Entry (POE) letter. This is not your work permit—you get the actual permit when you enter Canada.

### Step 6: Prepare for Arrival

Before flying to Canada:
1. Purchase health insurance covering your entire stay
2. Ensure your bank statement shows CAD $2,500+ (dated within 7 days of arrival)
3. Book your flight
4. Have proof of return transportation or funds for it

### Step 7: Enter Canada and Activate Your Permit

At the Canadian border:
1. Present your POE letter
2. Show your bank statement (must be less than 7 days old)
3. Show proof of health insurance
4. The border officer issues your work permit

If your insurance is only valid for 6 months, your work permit will only be issued for 6 months. Get insurance for the full year.

## The Hard Parts

### The Lottery (for Direct Applicants) / Limited RO Spots (for US Citizens)

If you were from the UK or Australia, you would enter the IEC pool and hope to get selected in a random draw. The lottery system means no guarantee of selection.

US citizens face a different problem: Recognized Organizations only have a limited allocation of spots. When those spots fill—through their own waitlist and selection process—there is no path forward for that year.

**SWAP's 2026 waitlist closed in late 2025.** If you missed it, you wait for 2027.

**The takeaway:** You must plan 6-12 months ahead. Apply to RO waitlists as soon as they open.

### High Upfront Costs

USD $3,500-5,500 before you earn anything. If your RO does not get you a spot, some fees may be refundable—but check their policies carefully.

### Timing Your Job Search

Most Working Holiday participants arrive without jobs lined up. This is normal and allowed under the open work permit.

**The challenge:** If you arrive in Whistler in November expecting ski season work but everything is already filled, you may struggle. Resort hiring happens months before the season.

**Timing matters:**
- Ski season hiring: July-September for November start
- Summer season hiring: February-April for May start
- Apply before you arrive or arrive early enough to job hunt in person

### Cost of Living in Popular Destinations

Vancouver and Whistler are expensive. Rent in Vancouver can consume most of a minimum-wage paycheck.

The Working Holiday financially works best if you:
- Work at a resort with staff housing
- Live with multiple roommates
- Work in a tipped position
- Stay somewhere besides Vancouver or Toronto

### One-Year Limit

The Working Holiday permit is valid for 12 months. You cannot extend it. After a year, you must leave Canada or transition to a different visa (student visa, work permit with employer sponsorship, etc.).

This is not a path to permanent residency on its own. It is a temporary work authorization for a defined period.

### Finding Legitimate Employers

Scammers target working holiday participants with fake job postings, requests for "processing fees," and too-good-to-be-true offers.

**Warning signs:**
- Job requires you to pay a fee upfront
- Communication only through personal email or WhatsApp
- Vague job description, guaranteed high pay
- Requests for passport or banking info before any interview

**How to verify employers:**
- Search the company name + "scam" or "+ reddit"
- Confirm the job exists on the employer's official website
- Call the employer directly using a number you find yourself (not one they provide)

## Proof of Funds

You must demonstrate at least CAD $2,500 in savings. Here is what the border officer needs to see:

- A bank statement dated within 7 days of your arrival
- Statement must show your name and account balance
- Can be a screenshot or printout from online banking
- Must show the equivalent of CAD $2,500 or more

If you arrive with CAD $10,000 or more, you must declare it to the border officer or face fines.

## Insurance Requirements

Health insurance is mandatory. Your policy must:

- Cover the entire duration of your intended stay
- Include medical care, hospitalization, and repatriation
- Be valid from your entry date

**If your insurance is shorter than your planned stay:** The border officer will issue a work permit that expires when your insurance expires. You cannot extend later. Buy 12-month coverage upfront.

**Insurance providers commonly used:**
- IEC Insurance (iecinsurance.com) — policies designed for Canadian working holidays
- SafetyWing — popular with travelers, but verify it meets IEC requirements
- World Nomads — check policy duration limits

Budget CAD $50-150/month for adequate coverage.

## Where This Can Lead

### During Your Year
- Build Canadian work experience for your resume
- Develop hospitality, customer service, or outdoor industry skills
- Make connections in the Canadian job market
- Explore whether you want to live in Canada long-term

### After Your Working Holiday
The Working Holiday does not lead directly to permanent residency. But it can position you for other pathways:

- **Employer sponsorship:** If an employer values you, they may sponsor a work permit
- **Provincial Nominee Programs:** Some provinces have pathways for workers with Canadian experience
- **Student visa:** If you decide to study in Canada, you have firsthand knowledge of the country
- **Return to the US:** With international work experience and possibly savings

### Skills That Transfer
- Working in multicultural environments
- Adaptability and problem-solving in unfamiliar situations
- Customer service across different contexts
- Self-reliance and independence
- Budget management while living abroad
