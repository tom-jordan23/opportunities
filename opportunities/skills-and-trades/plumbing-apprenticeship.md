# Plumbing Apprenticeship

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** Claude Code (Fact Checker Agent)

## Overview

A plumbing apprenticeship teaches you to install and repair water, gas, and drainage systems in buildings. The training takes 4-5 years, you earn while you learn from day one, and you graduate as a licensed journeyman plumber earning $60,000-$80,000 per year.

For young adults, plumbing offers a realistic path to financial stability without college debt. Union apprenticeships pay you during training. The work is in constant demand. People will always need toilets fixed and pipes installed. The industry faces a projected shortage of 550,000 plumbers by 2026 as older workers retire and too few young people enter the trade.

The honest reality: This is physically demanding work. You will crawl through tight spaces, dig trenches, and lift heavy materials. You will deal with sewage. The gross factor is real. Some days you will be elbow-deep in situations that would make most people gag. But if you can handle the physical demands and the occasional unpleasant job, plumbing offers something increasingly rare---a clear path to a middle-class income without a degree.

## What You'll Do

Plumbing work divides into three main categories. Where you work shapes what your days look like.

### New Construction

Construction plumbers install plumbing systems in buildings before anyone moves in. This is the cleanest plumbing work.

Daily tasks include:
- Reading blueprints and planning pipe layouts
- Cutting and fitting copper, PVC, and PEX pipes
- Installing drain lines, water supply lines, and gas pipes
- Setting fixtures---toilets, sinks, showers, water heaters
- Connecting systems to municipal water and sewer lines
- Working alongside electricians, HVAC techs, and other trades

The reality: Construction work is physical but predictable. You know what you are building. The work happens during regular daytime hours. The downside is exposure to weather and construction site conditions. You might work in freezing cold or extreme heat. New construction can slow down during recessions.

### Service and Repair

Service plumbers respond to problems in existing buildings. This is where you encounter the gross stuff.

Daily tasks include:
- Responding to emergency calls (burst pipes, backed-up sewers, no hot water)
- Diagnosing problems in systems you did not install
- Snaking drains and clearing clogs
- Repairing or replacing water heaters
- Fixing leaky faucets and running toilets
- Inspecting systems with cameras

The reality: Service work is unpredictable. You might unclog a simple drain one hour and wade into a flooded basement the next. You will see the inside of strangers' homes---some clean, some not. Emergency calls happen at night and on weekends. But service plumbers often earn more than construction plumbers because customers pay premium rates for emergencies.

### Commercial and Industrial

Commercial plumbers work on larger systems in hospitals, office buildings, factories, and institutions.

Daily tasks include:
- Installing and maintaining complex drainage systems
- Working with larger pipe sizes and industrial materials
- Coordinating with facility managers and building engineers
- Maintaining systems that serve hundreds or thousands of people
- Working on specialized systems (medical gas, laboratory waste)

The reality: Commercial work often pays more and involves larger-scale projects. The trade-off is more travel, larger crews, and sometimes strict regulations. Industrial plumbing crosses over with pipefitting.

### The Sewage Reality

Yes, you will deal with sewage. This is the part people worry about.

How often? It depends on your specialty. New construction plumbers rarely encounter sewage because the systems are not in use yet. Service plumbers dealing with drain clogs and sewer lines encounter it regularly.

What it is like: You develop a tolerance. One experienced plumber described it this way: "Eventually something inside of you dies and you'll be able to eat a sandwich while looking in a septic tank." That is dark humor, but it captures the truth. The work becomes routine. You wear gloves. You know what to avoid. It stops being shocking.

The protection: Modern plumbing tools---cameras, power snakes, and protective equipment---minimize direct contact. You are not wading through sewage daily. But you need to be mentally prepared for the times when things get messy.

## Requirements

**Age/Eligibility:**
- Minimum age: 18 years old (some programs accept 17-year-olds but cannot start until 18)
- High school diploma or GED required
- Valid driver's license
- Must pass drug screening
- Must pass background check
- Must be able to pass a physical examination

**Physical Requirements:**
- Ability to lift and carry 50-100 pounds regularly
- Kneeling, crawling, and working in confined spaces
- Working in trenches, crawl spaces, and attics
- Climbing ladders and scaffolding
- Standing for 8-10+ hour days
- Working outdoors in all weather conditions

The physical demands are real and unavoidable. Plumbing involves working in awkward positions---under sinks, in tight crawl spaces, inside walls. Over time, the lifting, bending, and kneeling take a toll on your body. Back injuries and knee problems are common among experienced plumbers. If you have existing mobility limitations or chronic back issues, discuss this honestly before committing.

**Skills Needed:**
- Basic math (measuring, calculating pipe lengths, reading blueprints)
- Mechanical aptitude (understanding how things connect and flow)
- Problem-solving ability (diagnosing issues in systems you cannot see)
- Ability to follow codes and regulations
- Customer service skills (for service work)

**Time Commitment:**
- Apprenticeship duration: 4-5 years (typically 8,000-10,000 hours of on-the-job training)
- Classroom hours: 144-246 hours per year (typically evenings or weekends)
- Full-time work during the day while attending classes

## Union vs. Non-Union Paths

Two main routes exist: union apprenticeships through the United Association (UA) or non-union apprenticeships with private employers.

### Union Apprenticeship (UA)

The United Association of Journeymen and Apprentices of the Plumbing and Pipe Fitting Industry of the United States and Canada represents plumbers, pipefitters, steamfitters, and sprinkler fitters.

**Advantages:**
- Structured 5-year training program with standardized curriculum
- Classroom training at union training centers (no tuition cost to you)
- Higher wages due to collective bargaining
- Health insurance and retirement benefits from day one
- Pension contributions
- Job placement assistance
- Industry-recognized credential upon completion
- 1,230+ classroom hours and 10,000 on-the-job hours

**Disadvantages:**
- Competitive application process (not everyone gets accepted)
- Application windows are limited (often once per year)
- May experience periods of unemployment between projects
- Union dues after completion ($30-$60/month typically)
- Less flexibility---you work where the union assigns you
- May require travel to different job sites

**How to apply:**
1. Find your local UA chapter at [ua.org](https://ua.org)
2. Check their apprenticeship application window (often March-April)
3. Prepare documents: birth certificate, high school transcript, valid driver's license
4. Take the aptitude test (often WorkKeys Assessment)
5. Complete the interview process
6. Pass physical exam and drug test if accepted

### Non-Union Apprenticeship

Many plumbing companies offer apprenticeships without union involvement. You work directly for a plumbing contractor who trains you.

**Advantages:**
- Often easier to get started (apply directly to companies)
- No application windows---hiring happens year-round
- More varied experience if the company does multiple types of work
- Direct relationship with employer
- May advance faster if you prove yourself
- More flexibility to change employers

**Disadvantages:**
- Quality of training varies widely by employer
- Lower wages typically ($15-$20/hour starting vs. $20-$30+ for union)
- Benefits may be limited or nonexistent
- No guaranteed classroom training
- Less standardized credential
- You may need to find your own path to licensure

**How to apply:**
1. Search Indeed, Craigslist, or local job boards for "plumber apprentice"
2. Call local plumbing companies directly
3. Ask at plumbing supply houses---they know who is hiring
4. Apply at multiple companies---persistence pays

### Which Path is Better?

Neither is universally better. It depends on your situation.

**Choose union if:** You want structured training, guaranteed benefits, higher wages, and can get accepted. You are willing to wait for an application window and go through the competitive process.

**Choose non-union if:** You want to start immediately, cannot get into the union, or prefer more flexibility. Plan to verify the quality of training and consider supplementing with trade school classes.

## Training Provided

Apprenticeship training combines on-the-job learning with classroom instruction.

**What you learn on the job:**
- Pipe cutting, threading, and joining (soldering, brazing, gluing)
- Installing fixtures (sinks, toilets, showers, water heaters)
- Reading blueprints and plumbing codes
- Diagnosing and repairing problems
- Working with different materials (copper, PVC, PEX, cast iron)
- Safety procedures and proper tool use

**What you learn in class:**
- Plumbing theory and physics of water systems
- Local and national plumbing codes
- Blueprint reading and drafting
- Mathematics for plumbing
- Safety regulations (OSHA)
- Welding and brazing (often)
- Business and customer relations

**Certifications earned:**
- State journeyman plumber license (after completing apprenticeship and passing exam)
- OSHA safety certifications
- Backflow prevention certification (additional specialty)
- Medical gas certification (additional specialty, higher pay)
- Some programs include welding certification

**Progression through years:**
- Year 1: Basic tasks, carrying materials, observing, learning tool names
- Year 2: Performing basic installations under supervision
- Year 3: More complex work, beginning to diagnose problems
- Year 4: Working more independently, preparing for journeyman exam
- Year 5: (if applicable) Advanced work, leadership responsibilities

## The Money Reality

### Pay During Apprenticeship

You earn while you learn. Apprentice wages start at roughly 40-50% of journeyman wages and increase each year.

**First-year apprentice wages (2025-2026):**
- National average: $15-$22/hour
- Union apprentices: $20-$30/hour in higher-paying regions
- Non-union apprentices: $15-$18/hour typical starting

**Wage progression example (Union):**
- Year 1: ~50% of journeyman rate ($20-$25/hour)
- Year 2: ~60% of journeyman rate
- Year 3: ~70% of journeyman rate
- Year 4: ~80% of journeyman rate
- Year 5: ~90% of journeyman rate

Annual earnings as a first-year apprentice: $31,000-$52,000 depending on location and union status. This increases each year.

### Journeyman Plumber Pay

After completing your apprenticeship and passing the journeyman exam:

**National figures (BLS May 2024 data):**
- Median annual wage: $62,970
- Bottom 10%: ~$40,000/year
- Top 10%: $105,000+/year
- Median hourly: ~$30/hour

**By location (2025-2026 estimates):**
- High-paying states (CA, NY, MA, DC): $70,000-$80,000+ average
- Texas average: ~$63,000
- Midwest average: $55,000-$65,000
- Rural areas: $45,000-$55,000

**Union vs. Non-Union:**
- Union journeymen: $35-$50/hour in many markets (plus benefits)
- Non-union journeymen: $25-$40/hour (benefits vary)

### Master Plumber Pay

After 5+ years as a journeyman, you can pursue a master plumber license:
- Median: ~$80,000/year
- With business ownership: $100,000-$200,000+ possible
- Supervisory roles: $75,000-$100,000

### Housing

Not provided. You are responsible for your own housing. The good news: journeyman wages support apartment rent or mortgage payments in most markets.

### Startup Costs

Apprenticeship training itself costs little or nothing. But you will need tools.

**Basic hand tool investment:**
- Starter toolkit: $200-$500
- Pipe wrenches (multiple sizes): $50-$150
- Pliers, screwdrivers, adjustable wrenches: $100-$200
- Measuring tape, level, flashlight: $50-$100
- Tool bag or box: $50-$150

**Total first-year tool cost: $300-$700**

Many employers provide specialty tools. Some union programs provide a basic toolkit. Ask before starting.

**Other costs:**
- Work boots (steel-toe required): $100-$200
- Work clothes that will get destroyed: $100-$200
- Reliable transportation to job sites: Variable (essential)
- Union application fee (if applicable): $25-$100

**What you avoid:** Unlike many paths, you do not pay tuition. Union apprenticeships include free classroom training. Non-union apprentices may need to take some trade school classes ($500-$2,000 for individual courses if required by state).

### Benefits (Union)

Union apprenticeships typically include:
- Health insurance (medical, dental, vision)
- Pension contributions
- Annuity/401(k) contributions
- Paid vacation after first year
- Paid holidays
- Life insurance
- Tool insurance

Non-union benefits vary dramatically by employer---some offer similar benefits, many offer little or nothing.

### Hidden Costs

- Ongoing tool replacement as things wear out or get lost
- Continuing education for license renewal
- Specialty certifications if you want to advance
- Work clothes replacement (the job is hard on clothing)
- Vehicle maintenance if driving to multiple sites

## How to Get Started

### Step 1: Decide union or non-union

Research UA locals in your area at [ua.org](https://ua.org). Click "Find a Local" and enter your location.

If a UA local exists nearby, find out:
- When is the next application window?
- What are the requirements?
- What is the aptitude test format?

If no union local exists or you cannot wait for the application window, plan the non-union route.

### Step 2: Gather documents

You will need:
- Birth certificate (or passport)
- High school diploma or GED
- High school transcript (for union applications)
- Valid driver's license
- Social Security card

### Step 3: Prepare for aptitude testing

Union apprenticeships require aptitude tests. The most common is the WorkKeys Assessment.

**Areas tested:**
- Applied mathematics
- Reading for information
- Locating information

**Study resources:**
- Free WorkKeys practice tests online
- Khan Academy for math review
- Local library resources

### Step 4: Apply

**For union:**
1. Apply during the published window (often March-April)
2. Complete aptitude testing
3. Interview with the apprenticeship committee
4. Pass physical and drug screening if selected
5. Wait for placement (could be immediate or several months)

**For non-union:**
1. Search job boards for "plumber apprentice" or "plumber helper"
2. Call plumbing companies directly: "Are you hiring apprentices?"
3. Visit plumbing supply houses and ask who is hiring
4. Apply to multiple companies---competition exists but persistence works
5. Show up presentable and ready to work hard

### Step 5: Get hired and start

Once accepted:
- Show up early every day
- Ask questions constantly
- Accept that you will do grunt work initially (carrying pipes, digging, cleaning up)
- Learn from journeymen who know more than you
- Stay humble---the learning curve is steep

## The Hard Parts

### Physical toll over time

The lifting, kneeling, crawling, and awkward positions accumulate. Back injuries and knee problems are occupational hazards. Plumbers in their 50s and 60s often have chronic pain from decades of physical work. Taking care of your body---stretching, proper lifting technique, knee pads, ergonomic tools---matters for career longevity.

### The gross factor

You will encounter human waste, rotting food debris, sewage backups, and mold. Some jobs are genuinely disgusting. Protective equipment helps, but you need mental preparation for unpleasant situations. If you have a weak stomach, this may be a dealbreaker.

### Unpredictable conditions

Construction sites mean exposure to weather---freezing cold, extreme heat, rain, mud. Service calls mean entering strangers' homes in unknown conditions. You cannot control your work environment.

### Customer interactions

Service plumbers deal directly with stressed homeowners. Some are grateful. Some are angry about costs. Some are unreasonable. You need patience and professionalism even when people are difficult.

### Seasonal and economic fluctuations

New construction slows during recessions. Union plumbers may experience layoffs between projects. Service work is more stable (pipes break regardless of the economy) but has its own pressures.

### Long apprenticeship

Five years is a significant commitment. You will watch friends in other paths advance while you are still an apprentice. The patience pays off---journeyman wages are worth the wait---but the timeline is real.

### Continuing education and licensing

Plumbing codes change. Licenses require renewal with continuing education. Some states require extensive hours for license maintenance. The learning never completely stops.

## Where This Can Lead

### Within Plumbing

**Journeyman Plumber** (after 4-5 year apprenticeship)
- Work independently
- Earn $60,000-$80,000+ annually
- Can specialize in areas you prefer

**Master Plumber** (after 5+ years as journeyman, varies by state)
- Additional licensing exam required
- Can supervise other plumbers
- Can pull permits
- Can start your own business
- Earns $75,000-$100,000+

**Plumbing Contractor/Business Owner**
- Requires master license in most states
- Unlimited income potential
- Also unlimited responsibility and risk
- Many successful plumbers eventually start their own companies

**Plumbing Inspector**
- Government position reviewing plumbing work for code compliance
- Steadier hours than field work
- Requires extensive experience and inspector certification
- Salary: $50,000-$80,000 typically

**Estimator/Project Manager**
- Transition from field work to office-based roles
- Calculate job costs and manage large projects
- Less physical demand as you age

### Related Trades

Your UA training may qualify you for related work:

**Pipefitter**
- Works with industrial piping systems in factories, power plants, refineries
- Higher-pressure systems, larger pipes
- Often higher pay than residential plumbing
- More travel, industrial settings

**Steamfitter**
- Specializes in high-pressure steam systems
- Power plants, heating systems, industrial facilities
- Requires additional training and certification

**Sprinkler Fitter**
- Installs fire suppression systems
- Less exposure to sewage
- Different apprenticeship track but related skills

**HVAC Technician**
- Many crossover skills with plumbing
- Works with heating and cooling systems
- Can combine plumbing and HVAC licenses

### Entrepreneurship

Plumbing is one of the most accessible paths to small business ownership. After achieving your master license:
- Low startup costs compared to other businesses (truck, tools, insurance)
- Steady demand for services
- Can start small (one person) and grow
- Successful plumbing business owners often earn $150,000-$300,000+
