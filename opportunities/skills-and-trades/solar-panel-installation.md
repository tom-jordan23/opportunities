# Solar Panel Installation

> **Status:** Verified
> **Last Verified:** 2026-02-09
> **Fact Checker:** Claude (Opus 4.5)

## Overview

Solar panel installation is one of the fastest-growing trades in the United States. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 42% job growth from 2024 to 2034. That growth rate outpaces nearly every other occupation in the country.

This path works well for someone in Sam's situation. Most employers hire entry-level workers with no experience and train them on the job. You start earning immediately. No upfront tuition or certification costs are required to get started.

The work is physically demanding. You spend most of your time on rooftops, often in extreme temperatures. Seasonal slowdowns and weather cancellations affect your hours and income. If you are uncomfortable with heights or heat, this is not the right path.

The solar industry faces policy uncertainty. Federal incentive programs have shifted, and some states offer more work than others. California, Texas, Florida, and Arizona have the largest markets. Research your local market before committing.

## What You'll Do

Solar photovoltaic installers assemble, install, and maintain solar panel systems on rooftops and other structures.

**A typical day includes:**
- Driving to residential or commercial job sites
- Carrying solar panels, racking, and equipment up ladders to rooftops
- Measuring and marking panel placement according to site plans
- Drilling and mounting racking systems to roofs
- Securing panels to the racking
- Running electrical conduit and wiring from panels to inverters
- Connecting systems to the electrical grid (if licensed or supervised)
- Testing systems for proper operation
- Cleaning up the job site

You work in crews of two to six people. Entry-level workers start by carrying materials, staging equipment, and assisting experienced installers. As you learn, you take on more technical tasks.

Most installations take one to three days for residential systems. Commercial projects run longer and involve larger crews.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** Must be 18 or older. Valid driver's license required. Must pass background check and drug screening. No specific citizenship requirements, but work authorization is required.

- **Skills Needed:** No prior experience required for entry-level positions. Basic math skills for measurements. Ability to read and follow diagrams and site plans. Comfort using hand and power tools. Construction, roofing, or electrical experience helps but is not required.

- **Physical Requirements:** This work is demanding. You must be able to:
  - Lift 50-70 pounds repeatedly
  - Carry awkward, heavy equipment up ladders
  - Work on sloped rooftops for hours at a time
  - Kneel, crouch, and work in confined spaces
  - Tolerate extreme heat, cold, and sun exposure
  - Maintain balance and stability at heights

- **Time Commitment:** Full-time work. Typically 40-50 hours per week during busy seasons. Hours fluctuate based on weather and demand. Some companies require weekend work.

## Training Provided

Most employers provide on-the-job training. This typically lasts one to twelve months depending on the company and your prior experience.

**What you'll learn on the job:**
- Solar PV system components and how they work
- Roof safety and fall protection
- Proper mounting and racking techniques
- Basic electrical connections
- System testing and troubleshooting
- Reading site plans and electrical diagrams

**Formal certifications:**
The North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) offers the industry's most recognized credentials.

- **NABCEP PV Associate:** Entry-level credential. Requires completing approved training or passing an exam. Exam costs $150. Demonstrates foundational knowledge.

- **NABCEP PV Installation Professional (PVIP):** Advanced certification. Requires 58+ hours of training plus documented work experience. Considered the gold standard for installers. Opens doors to higher pay and leadership roles.

Certification is not required to get hired. Many employers value hands-on experience equally or more. However, certification can increase your earning potential by $5,000-$15,000 per year and make you more competitive for promotions.

## The Money Reality

**Entry-level pay:**
- $16-$20 per hour ($33,000-$42,000 annually)
- Some companies pay piece rate per installation

**Mid-career pay (2-4 years experience):**
- $22-$28 per hour ($46,000-$58,000 annually)

**Experienced/Certified pay:**
- $28-$35 per hour ($58,000-$73,000 annually)
- Top earners reach $65,000-$75,000+

**Lead installers and foremen:**
- $55,000-$80,000 annually
- Installation managers can earn $80,000-$110,000

**Overtime:** Many companies offer overtime during busy seasons. This can add $5,000-$10,000 to annual income.

**Housing:** Not provided. You are responsible for your own housing near your employer's service area.

**Startup Costs:**
- Work boots with ankle support: $100-$200
- Basic hand tools: $200-$500 (some employers provide)
- Safety gear: Usually provided by employer
- Driver's license: Required
- OSHA 10 certification: $25-$50 (sometimes covered by employer)

**Hidden Costs:**
- Transportation to job sites (some companies provide work trucks, some require your own vehicle)
- Work clothes that you are willing to destroy
- Weather cancellations reduce hours and pay
- Seasonal layoffs in some regions during winter

**Benefits:** Vary widely by employer. Large companies like Sunrun offer health insurance, 401(k) matching, paid training, and tuition reimbursement. Smaller companies may offer fewer benefits. Ask about benefits during interviews.

## How to Get Started

**Step 1: Research your local market**
Solar installation is concentrated in certain states. California, Texas, Florida, Arizona, and Nevada have the most work. Check job listings in your area to gauge demand. If your region has few solar jobs, you may need to relocate.

**Step 2: Apply to entry-level positions**
Search for jobs with these titles:
- Solar Installer
- Solar Installation Technician
- Solar Panel Installer
- PV Installer
- Solar Laborer/Helper

Use these job sites:
- Indeed.com (search "solar installer entry level")
- Glassdoor.com
- Company career pages (Sunrun, Tesla Energy, Momentum Solar, Vivint Solar)
- ZipRecruiter.com

**Step 3: Apply to multiple companies**
Do not wait for one company to respond. Apply to every relevant opening. Large national companies hire continuously.

Major employers with entry-level programs:
- **Sunrun:** Largest residential installer. Offers paid training, tuition reimbursement, full benefits.
- **Note:** Research individual employers carefully. Some solar companies have aggressive sales cultures. Focus on installer positions with hourly pay rather than commission-based sales roles.
- **Tesla Energy:** Installations for Tesla solar products. Competitive pay.
- **Vivint Solar:** National installer with training programs.
- **Local and regional installers:** Often provide hands-on training and faster advancement.

**Step 4: Consider pre-apprenticeship or training programs**
If you want to build foundational skills before applying, consider:

- **GRID Alternatives SolarCorps Fellowship:** 11-month paid apprenticeship-model program. Hands-on training installing solar for low-income families. Available in California, Colorado, and Washington DC. Apply at gridalternatives.org.

- **GRID Alternatives Installation Basics Training (IBT):** Competency-based certificate program for entry-level skills. Contact local GRID office for availability.

- **Community college solar programs:** Some offer certificates in 6-12 weeks. Costs range from $1,000-$5,000. Verify the program includes hands-on installation training.

- **IBEW Union solar programs:** Some locals offer solar specialization tracks within electrician apprenticeships. This combines solar training with full electrician credentials.

**Step 5: Get OSHA 10 certification**
Many employers require OSHA 10-Hour Construction Safety certification. Complete this online for $25-$50 before applying. It shows employers you understand basic jobsite safety.

**Step 6: Build relevant experience**
If you have trouble getting hired, consider:
- General construction labor positions
- Roofing work (directly applicable skills)
- Warehouse work at electrical or solar supply companies
- Volunteer with GRID Alternatives installation days

## The Hard Parts

**Roof work is dangerous.** Falls are the leading cause of construction deaths. Nearly 38% of construction fatalities come from falls, slips, and trips. Rooftop solar installations increase this risk because workspace shrinks as you install more panels. You must be vigilant about safety every minute you are on a roof.

**The work is physically brutal.** You carry 40-50 pound panels up ladders repeatedly. You kneel on hot shingles. You work in 100-degree heat and freezing cold. You drill into roofs while maintaining balance on slopes. People with back, knee, or joint problems struggle in this work. Even healthy workers accumulate wear on their bodies over time.

**Weather controls your income.** Rain, snow, high winds, and extreme heat cancel work days. In northern states, winter months bring significant slowdowns. Some installers see 20-30% fewer hours in winter. Your paycheck fluctuates with the weather.

**Seasonal demand creates instability.** Spring and summer are peak installation seasons. Some companies hire aggressively in spring and lay off workers in fall. Ask employers directly about their seasonal patterns and whether they offer year-round employment.

**Heat exposure is relentless.** Solar panels heat up rapidly in sunlight. Rooftops are hotter than ground level. You work in direct sun for hours. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real risks. Hydration and breaks are essential but sometimes underemphasized by fast-moving crews.

**Electrical hazards are present.** Solar panels generate electricity when exposed to light. Improper handling can cause shocks or burns. Fatal electrocution is possible. You must follow safety protocols precisely.

**The industry depends on policy.** Federal and state incentives drive solar adoption. When incentives are strong, work is abundant. When incentives weaken or face uncertainty, companies slow hiring or reduce staff. The solar industry has experienced boom-and-bust cycles. This may affect long-term job security.

**Career advancement requires additional training.** Entry-level installation pays decently but has a ceiling. To earn significantly more, you need NABCEP certification, electrician credentials, or advancement to supervisory roles. These require investment of time and money.

**Job satisfaction is mixed.** Some installers love the outdoor work and find it rewarding. Others burn out from the physical demands and weather exposure. Industry surveys show solar thermal installers rate career happiness 3.1 out of 5 stars, placing them in the bottom 42% of careers for satisfaction.

## Where This Can Lead

**Within solar installation:**
- Lead Installer: Supervise installation crews. Requires 2-3 years experience.
- Foreman/Site Supervisor: Manage multiple crews and job sites. $55,000-$80,000.
- Installation Manager: Oversee all installations for a company. $80,000-$110,000.

**Specialized roles:**
- Solar System Designer: Design PV systems. Requires additional training.
- Sales/Project Development: Combine technical knowledge with customer-facing work.
- Commercial Solar: Larger projects with higher pay and more complexity.
- Battery Storage Specialist: Growing field as storage becomes standard.

**Related trades:**
- Electrician: Many solar installers pursue full electrician apprenticeships. This opens residential, commercial, and industrial electrical work beyond solar.
- HVAC: Some skills transfer. HVAC technicians work indoors more often.
- General Contractor: With experience and licensing, you can start your own installation company.

**Business ownership:**
Experienced installers with contractor licenses can start solar installation businesses. This requires business skills, capital, and licensing, but offers the highest earning potential. Successful solar contractors can earn well into six figures.
