## Overview

Walworth County's Public Works department hires **Highway Maintenance Workers**, and the postings are specific enough to plan around.

The county requires a **Wisconsin CDL Class A or B upon hire**. Employees hired with a Class B must **obtain a Class A within six months**, and must obtain a **Tanker Endorsement N within six months** as a condition of continued employment. The county provides a **$75 annual stipend for safety shoes** under county ordinance.

Note what that CDL language actually means: the county is willing to hire you with a Class B and let you upgrade on their timeline. That is a training pathway written into the job requirements.

**Verified pay:** Walworth County's Highway Maintenance Worker posting of December 2025 lists **$25.08 - $32.21 per hour**. A March 2025 posting for the same role listed $24.47 - $31.42, so the range is moving up. For comparison, Waukesha County's Patrol Worker in Highway Operations advertises $26.69-$35.31.

**An important correction, and the reason to read the next paragraph before getting excited.** The Walworth County posting requires **"two to three years of relevant prior experience"** in addition to the CDL. **This is not an entry-level hire-and-train position.** It is an excellent job with confirmed strong pay and a pension — but it is a target to work toward, not a door to walk through at 21 with no background.

The realistic entry points into this line of work are **municipal public works in the smaller villages**, **seasonal and limited-term county positions**, and getting the CDL first. Those are covered below, and the pay there is documented too.

Public works is one of the most reliably overlooked good jobs in any county. Stable, pensioned, outdoors, and it never gets outsourced.

## What You'll Do

The work follows the seasons, and that variety is one of the genuine appeals.

**Winter is snow and ice control**, and it dominates. Plowing routes, spreading salt and brine, and being called out at 2 a.m. when a storm arrives. This is the busiest and hardest season, and it is why the tanker endorsement matters — brine is a liquid load.

**Summer is construction and maintenance.** Asphalt patching and paving, shoulder work, culvert and ditch maintenance, sign installation and repair, guardrail work, pavement marking, mowing rights-of-way, and bridge inspection support.

**Year-round:** operating and maintaining trucks and heavy equipment, flagging and traffic control, storm damage response, and clearing downed trees.

You operate real equipment — plow trucks, loaders, backhoes, graders, sweepers, and chippers. Municipal public works crews in smaller villages do all of this plus water and sewer work, parks maintenance, and whatever else the village needs, which makes those jobs unusually varied.

The work is outdoors in Wisconsin weather, which means genuine cold and genuine heat. Traffic is a constant hazard — you work feet from moving vehicles.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+ for most positions; **21 is generally required for a CDL to drive interstate**, and some equipment roles require it. High school diploma or equivalent typically. Background check and DOT drug and alcohol testing program participation.
- **Licensing:** **Wisconsin CDL Class A or B upon hire** for Walworth County Highway Maintenance Worker. Class B hires must obtain **Class A within six months**, plus **Tanker Endorsement N within six months.** A DOT physical is required.
- **Experience — VERIFIED and important:** Walworth County requires **"high school diploma or equivalent competencies, and two to three years of relevant prior experience."** The experience requirement is real and it is the main barrier for a 21-year-old. Municipal and seasonal positions are generally less strict.
- **Skills Needed:** Mechanical aptitude and equipment experience. Willingness to work storms is essentially the core requirement.
- **Physical Requirements:** Lifting, shoveling, climbing on equipment, working outdoors in all conditions, and long hours during storm events.
- **Time Commitment:** Full-time, with **on-call and mandatory overtime during winter storms.** Limited-term and seasonal positions also exist and are a common route to permanent employment.

## Training Provided

**Equipment training is on the job**, and it is substantial. Learning to plow a route safely, operate a grader, or run a paving crew takes seasons, not weeks, and the county teaches it.

**The CDL is the interesting part.** Because Walworth County hires Class B holders and gives them six months to reach Class A, the upgrade happens while employed and paid. **Confirm whether the county pays for the upgrade training and testing** — that is the difference between a benefit and a personal expense.

**A specific local CDL training lead:** the Wisconsin DWD awarded Commercial Driver Training Grants in 2025, and **ECO CST Services LLC in Walworth County received $23,000 to provide Class A or B CDL training to 10 people.** That is a grant-funded, local CDL training provider — worth calling directly, and worth asking the Walworth County Job Center about.

Other training typically provided: flagger certification, confined space entry, traffic control, equipment-specific certifications, and first aid.

- **Housing:** Your own. Walworth County Public Works is in the county, so this is a short commute by definition.
- **Startup Costs:** **The CDL is the main one if you do not have it.** The county provides a **$75 annual safety shoe stipend.** Otherwise minimal — equipment and safety gear are provided.

- **Hidden Costs:** CDL training if unfunded — though see the grant-funded provider above and the [School Bus Driver](school-bus-driver-cdl.md) route to a free CDL.

## Hiring Pattern

Recurring. Walworth County has posted Highway Maintenance Worker positions repeatedly, including **full-time and limited-term** versions. Limited-term and seasonal roles are a well-established route into permanent county employment — take one, perform well, and apply internally.

**Employers in the radius:** Walworth County Public Works; Rock County Highway; Racine and Kenosha county highway departments; WisDOT; and every municipal public works department in the county — Lake Geneva, Elkhorn, Delavan, Whitewater, East Troy, Fontana, Williams Bay, Genoa City, Sharon, Darien.

**Village public works is the underrated version.** Small crews doing everything, often combined with [water and wastewater operator](water-wastewater-operator.md) duties, which means the village may pay for both your CDL and your DNR operator certification.

## How to Get Started

1. **Do not start with the county job.** It requires two to three years of relevant experience. Start with a **City of Whitewater, Lake Geneva, Elkhorn, or Delavan public works Laborer I position**, or a **seasonal laborer** role, and build the experience the county wants. Whitewater's Laborer I minimum ($25.98/hr) is actually higher than the county's entry rate.
2. **Check the Walworth County job portal** at governmentjobs.com/careers/walworthco anyway, and apply to **limited-term and seasonal** postings, which are less strict and are a documented route into permanent county employment.
3. **Get the CDL.** This is the other gating requirement and there are at least four ways to fund it: the county may pay for the Class A upgrade if you have a Class B; **ECO CST Services LLC in Walworth County** has DWD grant funding for CDL training; WIOA funding through the Walworth County Job Center; or the [school bus driver](school-bus-driver-cdl.md) route, which gets you a paid Class B.
4. **Call Walworth County Public Works directly** and ask whether they hire Class B holders and pay for the Class A upgrade, and whether they anticipate openings.
5. **Ask about the tanker endorsement** — who pays and on what timeline.
6. **Apply to village public works departments as the primary target**, not the fallback. Fewer applicants, lighter experience requirements, more varied work, and possible combined utility operator training.
7. **Check the Rock County and WisDOT postings** as well.
8. **Ask the Walworth County Job Center** about the DWD Commercial Driver Training Grant program and which local providers hold funding.
9. **Be explicit that you will work storms.** Winter availability is what they are actually hiring for, and saying so plainly matters.

## The Hard Parts

**Winter is the job, and winter is hard.** Called out at 2 a.m. into the storm everyone else is avoiding. Twelve to sixteen hour shifts during major events. Plowing is genuinely difficult driving — poor visibility, ice, and other drivers doing unpredictable things around a very large truck.

**Traffic is the real danger.** Highway workers are struck by vehicles. Drivers speed through work zones, look at phones, and misjudge distances. This is the leading occupational hazard and it does not go away.

**Weather exposure is constant.** Cold, wind, heat, and rain. There is no indoor version of this job.

**On-call restricts your winter.** Storm callouts mean staying available, sober, and near home during snow season.

**Physical wear.** Shoveling, lifting, climbing on equipment, and vibration from heavy machinery accumulate over a career.

**The CDL is a genuine barrier** if you do not have one and cannot get it funded. Solve that first.

**The experience requirement is the other barrier, and it is the bigger one.** Two to three years of relevant experience is not something you can study for. It means the county job is a two-or-three-year goal reached through municipal or seasonal work, not a first job.

**Public criticism.** Residents complain about road conditions, plowing, and construction, often to your face.

**Slow hiring.** County processes take time, and openings depend on retirements and budgets. Limited-term positions are the practical workaround.

## Where This Can Lead

A stable, pensioned career with a genuinely broad equipment skill set.

Progression: maintenance worker to equipment operator to crew leader, foreman, shop supervisor, and highway superintendent or public works director. County public works management is a well-paid position reachable without a degree.

**Sideways with real value:** the CDL and heavy equipment experience transfer directly to construction, excavation, trucking, and utility work. A Class A CDL with tanker endorsement plus years of equipment operation is a genuinely marketable combination.

**Adjacent municipal moves:** water and wastewater operator (frequently combined with public works in small villages), parks, and facilities.

**Private sector:** road construction contractors, excavating companies, and utility contractors all hire experienced operators, often at higher wages than county work — though without the pension.

Also worth naming: heavy equipment skills support self-employment in excavation, snow removal, and site work later on.
