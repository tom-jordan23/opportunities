## Overview

Generac makes backup generators and power equipment. Its Whitewater, Wisconsin facility is roughly 25 minutes from Walworth, and it appears to be hiring at volume — reported at **around 46 openings** at that location.

Reported entry rates: **1st shift assembly and painter positions around $19.85/hour**, with shift premiums of **+$2.50 for 2nd shift** and **+$3.00 for weekend shifts.** Benefits reported include medical, dental, and vision insurance plus paid time off and holidays.

Here is the detail that matters most, and it is easy to miss: **Generac posts Manufacturing Trainer positions** — roles whose stated purpose is preparing and delivering training to new manufacturing and production hires.

An employer that staffs dedicated trainers is an employer that actually trains. That is a much stronger signal than any recruiting language, and it is the reason this document exists. Most "we'll train you" claims are informal shadowing. A payroll line item for trainers is evidence.

## What You'll Do

Assembly and production work on generator manufacturing lines.

Assembly involves building units from components — mechanical fastening, wiring harnesses, mounting engines and alternators, installing control panels, and functional testing. Generators combine engines, electrical generation, and control electronics, which makes the work more varied than simple repetitive assembly and means you learn transferable mechanical and electrical skills.

Painter positions involve surface preparation and applying finish in a paint booth, working with coatings and the associated protective equipment.

Other production areas typically include welding and fabrication, machine operation, material handling, and quality inspection.

The pace is line-driven with production targets. Work is standing, hands-on, and repetitive within a station, though rotation between stations is common.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+. High school diploma or equivalent typically expected for production roles — confirm. Background check and drug screen standard.
- **Skills Needed:** None required for entry assembly. Mechanical aptitude and the ability to follow detailed work instructions matter. Reading a wiring diagram is a learnable advantage.
- **Physical Requirements:** Standing for full shifts, lifting, repetitive motion, working with hand and power tools. Paint work adds respirator use and chemical exposure controls.
- **Time Commitment:** Full-time. Multiple shifts including weekend shifts. Manufacturing overtime is common, and generator demand is seasonal — storm season and outage events drive production surges.

## Training Provided

**Structured, with dedicated trainers.** Generac's posted Manufacturing Trainer roles are responsible for preparing and delivering training to new manufacturing and production hires. That indicates a formal onboarding program rather than informal shadowing.

Expect training in plant-specific assembly procedures and work instructions, tool use, quality standards and inspection, safety including lockout/tagout, and lean manufacturing practices.

**What is not established** is whether Generac operates a registered apprenticeship or a defined internal path from production into maintenance, controls, or technician roles. That is the most valuable thing to ask about, because it is the difference between a job and a trajectory. Gateway Technical College and Blackhawk Technical College both run manufacturing apprenticeships that employers in this region sponsor — ask whether Generac participates.

Also ask about forklift certification and any welding certification, both of which are portable.

## How to Get Started

1. **Apply through Generac's own careers site**, not an aggregator. Aggregated postings for large manufacturers are frequently stale or agency-posted.
2. **Ask whether the position is direct hire or through a staffing agency.** Large manufacturers use temp-to-hire heavily. If it is temp, ask the conversion rate and timeline, and get the answer specifically.
3. **Ask why there are so many openings.** Growth and turnover are both plausible, and the answer tells you a great deal. Ask what the annual turnover rate is on the line you would join.
4. **Ask about the training program directly.** You know they employ Manufacturing Trainers — reference it. Ask how long structured training lasts and who delivers it.
5. **Ask about the path from production into maintenance or technician roles**, and whether Generac sponsors registered apprenticeships through Gateway or Blackhawk. This is the question that separates a job from a career.
6. **Ask about the seasonal demand pattern** and what it means for hours.
7. **Compare against Kikkoman before deciding.** Kikkoman is reported at $21.81 and is ten minutes away; Generac 1st shift is reported at $19.85 and is twenty-five minutes away. Generac's advantage is the training infrastructure and a larger internal ladder. Weigh both.
8. **Ask the Walworth County Job Center** whether Generac has an OJT contract.

## The Hard Parts

**Forty-six openings is ambiguous, and you should find out which it means.** High turnover at a manufacturer usually reflects something — pace, management, mandatory overtime, or pay relative to local alternatives. Ask directly and read reviews before accepting.

**Assembly line work is repetitive by design.** Standardized work means doing the same operation to a cycle time. Station rotation helps. It is still repetitive.

**Production targets create pressure.** Line speed is set by the plan, not by how you feel that day.

**Seasonal swing.** Generator demand spikes with storms and grid instability. That can mean mandatory overtime in busy periods and reduced hours in slow ones. Ask how the last two years actually went.

**Paint work carries real exposure.** Solvents and coatings require respirators and discipline about protective equipment. Take it seriously every shift.

**Physical accumulation.** Standing, lifting, and repetitive motion over years produce genuine injuries.

**The commute in winter.** Twenty-five minutes on a clear day; longer on a January morning after snow, on rural roads, for a shift that starts before dawn.

**Plant-specific skills.** As with all production work, what you learn is largely specific to this employer unless you deliberately pursue portable certifications.

## Where This Can Lead

Inside Generac: line lead, quality inspector, team lead, and supervisor. More valuably, **maintenance technician, controls technician, and test technician** — these roles pay substantially more, and generator manufacturing involves engines, electrical generation, and control systems, which makes it good background for all three.

Generac is also a large company with multiple Wisconsin sites, which means internal transfer is a real option — different plant, different shift, different department.

Outside: this corridor has a dense manufacturing base, and assembly experience with a clean attendance record transfers readily. The genuinely high-value moves are into industrial maintenance, welding with certifications, CNC machining, or quality and inspection work.

**Treat the registered apprenticeship question as the main opportunity here.** Gateway's Industrial Manufacturing Technician apprenticeship trains for 1.5 years with paid related instruction and reports earnings up to $30.33/hour as an established manufacturing technician. If Generac sponsors that, this job becomes a genuine entry point rather than a paycheck.
