# 911 Telecommunicator / Dispatcher

> **Status:** Draft
> **Last Verified:** Not yet fact-checked — drafted 2026-08-26
> **Fact Checker:** Pending

## Overview

This is one of the best-paying jobs in this category that requires nothing before you apply, and almost nobody young considers it.

A 911 telecommunicator answers emergency calls, decides what is happening, and dispatches police, fire, and ambulance. You are the first person in the chain. Everything that happens afterward depends on the information you get and how fast you get it.

The reason it belongs in this category is that counties train you completely. Walworth County states it provides initial on-the-job training plus Wisconsin TIME System certification, Emergency Medical Dispatcher certification, and Public Safety Telecommunicator 1. You arrive with a high school diploma and typing speed. They build the rest.

Rock County's 2025 starting wage for Telecommunicator was **$26.25/hour**, with Call Taker at **$22.05/hour**. For a job with no prior credential requirement, in this region, that is a strong number — and it comes with a public pension.

It suits someone who stays calm when other people are losing it, and who can hold several things in their head at once.

## What You'll Do

You sit at a console with multiple screens and a radio, wearing a headset, for a full shift.

Calls arrive without warning and without context. A caller may be screaming, whispering, confused, drunk, a child, or silent. Your job is to determine location first, then nature of the emergency, then dispatch the right units — while continuing to gather information and often while giving instructions. Emergency Medical Dispatcher certification means you will talk people through CPR and childbirth over the phone.

Between calls you run license plates and warrant checks for officers on the road, monitor radio traffic from multiple agencies, log everything, and handle non-emergency calls about barking dogs and parking.

The multitasking is the actual skill. You may be typing one incident, talking to a caller about a second, and listening to radio traffic about a third, simultaneously.

Shifts are typically twelve hours, rotating, covering nights, weekends, and holidays. The center never closes.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+. High school diploma or equivalent. Extensive background investigation — this is a law enforcement adjacent position, and the background check is thorough. No disqualifying criminal history. Drug screen. Some agencies require a polygraph or psychological evaluation.
- **Skills Needed:** **Typing speed is a hard gate.** Wisconsin agencies commonly require a minimum of 31 words per minute; Rock County requires **35 net words per minute** and a passing score on the **CritiCall** computerized multitasking exam before you reach an interview.
- **Physical Requirements:** Sitting for long shifts. Hearing must be good. The physical toll is fatigue and stress rather than exertion.
- **Time Commitment:** Full-time, shift work, rotating. Mandatory overtime is common because centers are chronically short-staffed.

## Training Provided

Fully paid and comprehensive. This is a genuine hire-and-train role with recognized certifications attached.

Walworth County states it provides:
- Initial on-the-job training
- **Wisconsin TIME System certification** — access to state and federal criminal justice databases
- **Emergency Medical Dispatcher (EMD) certification** — protocol-driven medical instruction over the phone
- **Public Safety Telecommunicator 1 (PST1)** — the foundational national certification for the role

Training typically runs several months, combining classroom instruction with supervised console time alongside an experienced trainer before you take calls alone.

The certifications are portable. A trained telecommunicator can work in any dispatch center in the state, and PST1 and EMD are recognized nationally.

## The Money Reality

- **Pay:** Rock County 2025 starting wages — **Telecommunicator $26.25/hour**, **Call Taker $22.05/hour**. Walworth County advertises a competitive salary but the figure is still unconfirmed.
- **Municipal benchmark — VERIFIED:** the **City of Whitewater 2026 Salary Resolution** (effective 1/1/2026) lists **Dispatcher I in Grade H at $22.47 minimum / $26.40 mid / $30.34 maximum** per hour, and **Dispatcher II in Grade G at $20.90 / $24.55 / $28.21.** (Whitewater grades Dispatcher I above Dispatcher II, which is unusual naming — confirm which is the entry title before applying.) A small-city dispatch center pays less than a county 911 center, which is consistent with Rock County's higher figure. Shift differentials for nights and weekends typically add to base. Overtime is frequent and often mandatory, which raises annual earnings substantially.
- **Housing:** Your own.
- **Startup Costs:** Effectively zero. No tuition, no certification to buy, no tools. This is one of the few genuinely free entry points.
- **Benefits:** Walworth County lists health, dental, and vision insurance, an on-site fitness room at the Sheriff's Office, and a tuition reimbursement program. County employment in Wisconsin generally includes participation in the **Wisconsin Retirement System** — a real pension, which almost nothing else in this category offers.
- **Hidden Costs:** None financially. The cost is paid in sleep and stress.

## Hiring Pattern

Recurring, through eligibility lists. Walworth County has posted both specific Communications Officer openings and a "Communications Officer Eligibility List" — meaning they test and qualify candidates into a pool, then hire from it as vacancies occur.

**This changes your strategy.** Do not wait for a posting that matches your timing. Apply to the eligibility list whenever it is open, pass the testing, and be in the pool when a seat opens.

Dispatch centers in the radius: Walworth County Sheriff's Office, Rock County Communications Center, Racine County, Kenosha County, Winnebago County (Rockford IL), McHenry County IL, and several municipal centers.

## How to Get Started

1. **Check the Walworth County job portal now** at governmentjobs.com/careers/walworthco and search for "Communications Officer." Apply to any open posting *or* eligibility list.
2. **Check the Rock County 911 Communications Center employment page** at co.rock.wi.us — they post their own openings and wage scales.
3. **Test your typing speed today.** Free online tests take one minute. If you are below 35 net words per minute, practice daily — this is the most common reason applicants are eliminated, and it is completely fixable in a few weeks.
4. **Practice for CritiCall.** It tests multitasking, data entry, memory, and map reading under time pressure. Practice materials exist online. Candidates who walk in cold usually fail.
5. **Clean up your background before you apply.** Résumés matter less here than the background investigation. Be honest on the application — undisclosed issues fail people more often than the issues themselves.
6. **Ask for a tour or a sit-along.** Many centers allow observation. It is the fastest way to learn whether you can tolerate the environment, and it demonstrates serious interest.
7. **Apply to several counties at once.** Walworth, Rock, Racine, Kenosha, and Winnebago all hire independently.

## The Hard Parts

**You hear the worst moments of people's lives, and you rarely learn how they end.** Child deaths, suicides, fatal crashes, domestic violence. You handle the call, then take the next one. The lack of resolution is what wears people down more than the calls themselves.

**Burnout and turnover are high across the profession.** This is well documented and is the main reason centers are always hiring.

**Rotating shift work damages sleep and social life.** Twelve-hour nights, weekends, and holidays. Missing family events is routine, not occasional.

**Mandatory overtime.** Short-staffed centers hold people over. You may not be able to leave when your shift ends.

**You will make a mistake that matters.** Everyone does. Wrong address, delayed dispatch, missed detail. Learning to carry that without being destroyed by it is the real job requirement.

**The hiring process is slow and invasive.** Testing, background investigation, sometimes polygraph and psychological evaluation. Months, not weeks.

**It is sedentary and isolating.** Long hours in a dim room with a headset on.

**Get honest about the emotional load before applying.** This is not a job to try casually. Read the subreddit, take a sit-along, and be truthful with yourself about it.

## Where This Can Lead

Inside dispatch: senior telecommunicator, communications training officer, shift supervisor, communications center manager. Wisconsin county government has a clear internal ladder, and the pension grows with it.

Sideways into public safety: many dispatchers become police officers, deputies, or paramedics. Dispatch experience is regarded as strong preparation and gives you inside knowledge of the agency you are applying to.

Related fields: emergency management, hospital communications, utility and railroad dispatch, air traffic control (a separate and demanding path, but dispatch experience is relevant).

The transferable skills are genuinely valuable and unusual — decision-making under pressure, structured communication, and multitasking that most people cannot do.
