# WIOA On-the-Job Training (Walworth County Job Center)

> **Last Verified:** Not yet fact-checked — drafted 2026-08-26
> **Fact Checker:** Pending

## Overview

This is not a job. It is the mechanism that gets you hired into one.

Under the federal Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act, a local workforce board can pay an employer to hire and train someone with no experience. The board reimburses the employer for a share of your wages during the training period. You are a normal employee on a normal paycheck. The employer is the one receiving the subsidy.

Understanding this changes how you look for work. Most people apply only to postings that say "no experience necessary," which is a small and crowded pool. Instead, you become an eligible participant first. Then the board can approach employers on your behalf with money attached. You stop asking for a favor and start bringing a subsidy.

The Walworth County Job Center in Elkhorn states that it offers assistance with on-the-job, classroom, and short-term training in high-demand careers. It also offers free career and skill assessments.

This document is listed first in the category on purpose. Read it before the others.

## What You'll Do

The process is unglamorous and mostly involves talking to people.

You visit the job center in person. Staff interview you about your work history, your interests, and your constraints. You take a skills assessment if you want one — it is free and it is genuinely useful if you do not yet know what you are good at. Staff determine whether you qualify for WIOA-funded services.

If you qualify, a career planner works with you to identify a target occupation. From there the board may do any of several things: place you into an OJT contract with an employer, fund a short-term certification course, connect you to a registered apprenticeship sponsor, or simply coach you through applications.

You will need to follow up. Workforce boards are busy and underfunded. The people who get the most out of this system are the ones who show up, call back, and stay in contact.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** Adult WIOA services generally require you to be 18 or older and authorized to work in the United States. Selective Service registration is typically required for men. Eligibility rules have priority categories, and funding is limited — being eligible does not guarantee enrollment.
- **Skills Needed:** None. That is the point.
- **Physical Requirements:** None for the program itself. The job it places you in may have plenty.
- **Time Commitment:** An initial visit takes a few hours. Enrollment can take a few weeks. An OJT contract typically runs weeks to months depending on the occupation.
- **Documents to bring:** Photo ID, Social Security card, proof of address, and if you have them, a high school diploma or GED and a resume. Bring more than you think you need.

## Training Provided

Whatever the OJT contract specifies. That is defined per employer and per occupation, and it is written into the agreement.

The important structural detail is that WIOA-funded training is documented. There is a training plan, a set of skills to be taught, and a defined period. That is meaningfully better than an informal "we'll show you the ropes," because someone outside the employer is checking whether the training actually happened.

Boards also fund short-term classroom training through the Eligible Training Provider List. If a certification is genuinely required for a target job — a CDL, a certified nursing assistant course, a forklift certification — the board may pay for it rather than you.

## The Money Reality

- **Pay:** You are paid the job's normal wage by the employer. The subsidy goes to the employer, not to you, and it should not reduce what you earn.
- **Housing:** Not provided. You live at home. That is the entire premise of this category.
- **Startup Costs:** Effectively zero. Job center services are free. Budget gas money for trips to Elkhorn.
- **Benefits:** Whatever the hiring employer offers. Support services — help with transportation, work clothes, or tools — are sometimes available through the board. Ask specifically.
- **Hidden Costs:** Your time. Enrollment involves paperwork and waiting.

## Hiring Pattern

Continuous. The Walworth County Job Center is open Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 4:30 pm.

Whether *active OJT contracts currently exist, and with which employers*, changes constantly and is not published. That question can only be answered by asking. It is the single most valuable question in this document.

## How to Get Started

1. **Go to the Walworth County Job Center in person.** 400 County Rd H, Elkhorn, WI 53121. (262) 741-5180. Monday-Friday, 8:00 am - 4:30 pm. Walking in works better than calling.
2. **Ask this exact question:** "I want on-the-job training. What OJT contracts do you have with employers right now, and what would I need to do to qualify?"
3. **Ask for the free skills assessment.** It costs nothing and it will either confirm a direction or save you from a wrong one.
4. **Also visit the Rock County Job Center in Janesville.** Walworth County is served by Southeastern WoRKs. Rock County is served by a different board — the Southwest Wisconsin Workforce Development Board. Different funding, different employer relationships. There is no rule against working with both.
5. **Ask about the Eligible Training Provider List.** If a short certification would unlock a job, ask whether the board will fund it.
6. **Create a Job Center of Wisconsin account** at wisconsinjobcenter.org and set alerts for Walworth, Rock, Racine, and Kenosha counties.
7. **Follow up every two weeks.** Persistence is the variable you control.

## The Hard Parts

**Funding is limited and rationed.** Being eligible is not the same as being enrolled. Boards prioritize certain groups, and money runs out before the need does.

**It is slow.** Weeks, sometimes longer. If you need income immediately, take a job now and work the job center process in parallel. Do not wait on it.

**Nobody chases you.** Career planners handle large caseloads. The applicants who disappear get forgotten, and that is not malice — it is arithmetic.

**Offerings depend on which employers the board has relationships with.** If no local employer currently has an OJT contract in a field you want, the board cannot conjure one quickly.

**Government offices can feel discouraging.** Fluorescent lights, waiting, forms. Go anyway. The value here is real and almost nobody uses it.

## Where This Can Lead

Straight into most of the other documents in this category. An OJT contract is a delivery mechanism, not a destination.

It also produces two durable assets. The first is a documented training record, which is evidence for the next employer. The second is a relationship with a career planner who knows the local employer landscape better than any job board does.
