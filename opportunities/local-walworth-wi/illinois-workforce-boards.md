# Illinois-Side Workforce Boards (Rockford & McHenry)

## Overview

Walworth sits eight miles from the Illinois line, and that border is worth crossing on paper as well as by car.

Wisconsin and Illinois run **separate WIOA funding streams with separate rules and separate money.** The [Walworth County Job Center](wioa-on-the-job-training.md) is one system. The Illinois boards south of the line are another, and they publish more specific numbers than the Wisconsin side does.

**The Workforce Connection** serves Boone, Stephenson, and Winnebago counties — the Rockford area. Its **on-the-job training program reimburses businesses 50% to 75% of a new employee's wages for up to six months**, and the board helps the employer build the training plan.

**McHenry County Workforce Network** covers the Illinois communities closest to Walworth. If you qualify for WIOA there, the network **can cover up to $7,000 for non-medical training and up to $10,000 for medical training.**

Those are unusually concrete figures. A board that will put $10,000 toward a medical credential, or reimburse an employer three-quarters of your wage for half a year, is a serious lever — and almost nobody thinks to ask on both sides of a state line.

**A caution up front, now partly resolved.** Eligibility is generally tied to residency or to where you intend to work. Checking the general WIOA framework produced a useful refinement: **residency requirements are set at the state and local level, not by federal law.** Some states explicitly forbid local boards from imposing a residency requirement or preference. Some boards serve out-of-area residents **on a case-by-case basis with approval, coordinated with the workforce board of the person's residence.** Others do require residency in the local area.

So the honest answer is not "no." It is **"it depends on that board's policy, and out-of-area service is a recognized arrangement that boards handle through coordination."** That is a much better position from which to make the call — you are asking about a known process rather than asking for an exception. **Ask specifically: "What is your policy on serving out-of-area residents, and can you coordinate with my home board in Walworth County, Wisconsin?"**

## What You'll Do

Nothing, until you visit. This is infrastructure, not a job.

The process mirrors the Wisconsin side. You go to an American Job Center location, staff assess your work history and goals, they determine WIOA eligibility, and if you qualify a career planner works with you on a plan. That plan may involve an OJT contract with an employer, funded short-term training, or job search assistance.

The difference is the specificity of what is on offer. The Rockford board publishes a wage reimbursement range. The McHenry network publishes training funding caps. Those numbers give you something concrete to ask about.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** Adult WIOA services generally require you to be 18 or older and authorized to work. Selective Service registration is typically required for men. **Residency or intent-to-work rules are the open question for a Wisconsin resident** — ask directly.
- **Skills Needed:** None.
- **Physical Requirements:** None.
- **Time Commitment:** An initial visit takes a few hours. Enrollment takes weeks.
- **Documents to bring:** Photo ID, Social Security card, proof of address, high school diploma or GED if you have one, and a resume.

## Training Provided

Whatever the plan specifies. Two mechanisms matter most:

**On-the-Job Training contracts (The Workforce Connection).** The board reimburses an employer **50% to 75% of your wages for up to six months** while they train you, and supports the creation of a written training plan. You are a normal paid employee throughout. That reimbursement rate is generous enough to change an employer's hiring math substantially — it is the difference between "we can't afford to train someone" and "yes."

**Training grants (McHenry County Workforce Network).** For eligible individuals, **up to $7,000 for non-medical training and up to $10,000 for medical training.** That is enough to fund a CDL, a CNA-to-LPN bridge, a phlebotomy or sterile processing program, an IT certification track, or a machine tool course outright.

Both route through approved training provider lists, so the specific programs available are defined rather than open-ended.

## The Money Reality

- **Pay:** Not applicable directly. Under an OJT contract you earn the job's normal wage from the employer.
- **Housing:** Your own.
- **Startup Costs:** Zero. These are free public services. Budget gas for trips to Rockford or McHenry County.
- **Benefits:** Support services — transportation assistance, work clothing, tools — are sometimes available. Ask specifically.
- **Hidden Costs:** Time, paperwork, and the drive.

## Hiring Pattern

Continuous. These are year-round agencies.

The variable is which employers currently hold OJT contracts, which is not published anywhere and changes constantly. That question can only be answered by asking.

## How to Get Started

1. **First, call and ask the eligibility question in the right form.** Not "am I eligible?" but: **"What is your policy on serving out-of-area residents, and can you coordinate with my home workforce board in Walworth County, Wisconsin?"** Out-of-area service through inter-board coordination is a recognized arrangement, and asking about it by name gets a better answer than asking generally.
2. **Contact The Workforce Connection** in Rockford. Ask what OJT contracts they currently hold and with which employers, and confirm the 50-75% reimbursement range and six-month duration.
3. **Contact McHenry County Workforce Network.** Ask about the training grant, confirm the $7,000 and $10,000 caps, and ask which approved programs they will fund.
4. **Ask both boards for their approved training provider lists.** That tells you exactly what they will pay for.
5. **Ask about medical training funding specifically at McHenry.** The higher $10,000 cap for medical training aligns well with several paths in this category — [sterile processing](sterile-processing-technician.md), [phlebotomy](phlebotomy-patient-care-tech.md), and CNA-to-LPN routes.
6. **Do the Wisconsin side in parallel.** Visit the [Walworth County Job Center](wioa-on-the-job-training.md) in Elkhorn regardless. There is no rule against exploring both systems, and they fund different things.
7. **Note the geography.** Rockford employers reached through The Workforce Connection are about 50 minutes away. McHenry County is closer — Harvard and Richmond are 25-35 minutes. Weigh the commute against the funding.

## The Hard Parts

**Cross-state eligibility is still not settled, though it is not a flat no.** WIOA funding is allocated by geography, and residency rules are set at state and local level. Some boards serve out-of-area residents case-by-case with inter-board coordination; some require local residency. A Wisconsin resident may qualify, may qualify only when working for an Illinois employer, or may not qualify at all — it depends on that board's written policy. **Find out before building a plan around it.**

**Funding is limited and rationed everywhere.** Published caps are maximums, not entitlements. Boards prioritize certain groups and money runs out.

**It is slow.** Weeks to enroll, longer to place. Not a solution if you need income next week.

**The drive is real.** Rockford is roughly 50 minutes. Attending appointments there is a genuine time cost, and any job placed through that board is likely to be in that same area.

**Nobody follows up with you.** Career planners carry heavy caseloads. Persistence is the variable you control.

**Two systems means twice the paperwork.** Working both sides is worth it, and it is administratively tedious.

## Where This Can Lead

Into any of the other documents in this category, with funding attached.

The specific strategic value of the Illinois side is the **published training grant amounts.** Wisconsin's ETPL funding exists but the sources reviewed did not publish per-person caps. McHenry County states $7,000 and $10,000. If a credential is the obstacle — a CDL, a CNA, a sterile processing certificate, an IT certification — a board that will write that check changes the calculation entirely.

The other value is the **Rockford OJT reimbursement rate.** At 50-75% of wages for six months, that is a strong enough incentive that an employer who would never hire an untrained 21-year-old might reconsider.
