# Mercyhealth Entry-Level & Nursing Assistant Training Program

> **Status:** Draft
> **Last Verified:** Not yet fact-checked — drafted 2026-08-26
> **Fact Checker:** Pending
> **Hiring Pattern:** Continuous. Large multi-site system with steady entry-level turnover. CNA training runs in cohorts with regular graduations.

## Overview

Mercyhealth is the most important single employer in this category, for one simple reason: **it operates a hospital in Walworth itself**, plus facilities in Lake Geneva, Janesville, Beloit, and Rockford. Every ring of the radius is covered by one employer with one application system.

It also runs its own **Nursing Assistant Training Program** at the Mercyhealth Hospital and Trauma Center campus in Janesville — a reported **88-hour program** delivered either as **12 classes over five weeks** or in an accelerated **four-week format of 8 lecture days and 4 clinical days**, with students rotating through diverse hospital departments for clinical experience. The program graduates cohorts regularly.

A CNA credential is the master key to healthcare in this region. It qualifies you for patient care technician roles, satisfies the "CNA preferred" line on orthopedic technician postings, and is the standard prerequisite for moving into nursing later with employer tuition support.

**The critical unknown:** whether the training program is free, whether trainees are paid, and whether completion comes with a job offer. Those three answers determine everything, and they are one phone call away.

## What You'll Do

Depends entirely on the role, and Mercyhealth has many entry points.

**Certified Nursing Assistant / Patient Care Technician.** Direct patient care under nurse supervision — bathing, toileting, feeding, repositioning, transferring patients, taking vital signs, and documenting. It is physically demanding, intimate work with people at their most vulnerable. It is also the fastest way to find out whether healthcare is genuinely for you.

**Environmental services, dietary, patient transport, and unit clerk.** Support roles that get you inside the building with an employee badge, internal transfer rights, and access to tuition benefits. Less glamorous, genuinely valuable as a starting position.

**Sterile processing, phlebotomy, and monitor tech.** Technical support roles — see [Sterile Processing Technician](sterile-processing-technician.md) and [Phlebotomy & Patient Care Tech](phlebotomy-patient-care-tech.md).

Hospitals run continuously. Expect evening, overnight, weekend, and holiday shifts, particularly when starting out.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+ for most clinical roles. High school diploma or equivalent. Background check, drug screen, immunizations including annual influenza and typically hepatitis B, and a TB test.
- **Skills Needed:** None for support roles. The CNA training program teaches the clinical skills. What matters is reliability, patience, and a stomach for bodily fluids.
- **Physical Requirements:** For direct patient care, substantial. Lifting and transferring patients, standing entire shifts, and repetitive bending. Back injuries are the occupational hazard of this work.
- **Time Commitment:** Full-time and part-time both available. The CNA training program is a four to five week commitment before the job starts.

## Training Provided

**The Nursing Assistant Training Program** is the centerpiece. Reported structure:
- **88 hours total**
- **12 classes over a five-week span**, or an accelerated **four-week format** with 8 days of lecture and 4 days of clinical
- Clinical rotations through multiple departments at the Janesville hospital campus
- Prepares you for Wisconsin CNA certification

Mercyhealth also runs nursing career advancement programming aimed at helping non-nurses take the first step toward nursing by becoming a CNA — which signals the CNA program is deliberately built as a pipeline rather than a standalone course.

**Beyond CNA:** hospital systems this size typically offer tuition assistance, internal transfer preference, and clinical ladder programs. Those benefits are the real long-term value of hospital employment and are worth more than a dollar an hour at hire.

**Ask about the three things that matter:** Is the program free or is there tuition? Are training hours paid? Does completion come with a guaranteed position, or only eligibility to apply?

CNA pay in this region generally runs in the high teens to low twenties per hour, with shift differentials for evenings, nights, and weekends. **Verify against live postings — this is a significant gap.**
- **Training cost:** **Still unknown, and still the most important open question in the document.** Mercyhealth's nursing career advancement page returned HTTP 403 when fetched on 2026-08-26, so this could not be resolved from public sources. **It requires a phone call** — and it is the single most valuable call on the list, because the three possible models (free with a work commitment, employer-paid with wages, or tuition-charging) differ enormously in value. Some hospital CNA programs are free with a work commitment; some charge tuition; some pay you as an employee throughout. The three models are worth very different amounts.
- **Housing:** Your own. **Mercyhealth's Walworth facility means a commute of minutes, not an hour** — a real advantage no other large employer here offers.
- **Startup Costs:** Low. Scrubs and shoes. Possible fees for the CNA state certification exam and background check. Ask who pays.
- **Benefits:** Mercyhealth advertises retirement, paid time off, on-demand pay, and an Employee Assistance Program. Health insurance for benefit-eligible positions. Ask about tuition assistance specifically — for a 21-year-old, that may be the single most valuable benefit on the list.
- **Hidden Costs:** Certification renewal. Required annual immunizations, usually employer-provided.

## Hiring Pattern

Continuous across a large system. Mercyhealth operates in Walworth, Lake Geneva, Janesville, Beloit, and Rockford, which means one employer covers every ring of this radius, and internal transfer between sites becomes possible once you are inside.

The Nursing Assistant Training Program runs in cohorts with regular graduations, so there is an application cycle to catch rather than a continuously open door.

## How to Get Started

1. **Call Mercyhealth about the Nursing Assistant Training Program and ask exactly three questions:** Does it cost anything? Are the training hours paid? Does completing it guarantee a position, or only the right to apply? Everything else depends on those answers.
2. **Complete the program application** at the Mercyhealth forms link below, or ask HR for the current cohort schedule.
3. **Apply directly at careers.mercyhealthsystem.org** — do not use aggregators, which carry stale postings for large systems.
4. **Filter for the Walworth and Lake Geneva sites first.** A ten-minute commute changes the economics of a $19/hour job substantially compared with driving to Rockford.
5. **Apply to support roles in parallel** — environmental services, dietary, transport, unit clerk. Getting inside the building with a badge is worth more than waiting for the perfect opening, because internal candidates have real advantages.
6. **Ask about tuition assistance at the interview**, including how long you must work before becoming eligible. At 21 this is the most valuable thing the employer can offer you.
7. **Ask the Walworth County Job Center** whether WIOA will fund CNA training as an alternative — CNA programs are commonly on the Eligible Training Provider List.
8. **Say you will take nights and weekends.** It is the fastest way in and it pays a differential.

## The Hard Parts

**CNA work is physically and emotionally heavy.** You lift and transfer adults who cannot move themselves. You clean people who have soiled themselves. You are present for deaths. It is genuinely hard work that is genuinely undervalued, and the pay does not reflect the difficulty.

**Back injuries are the occupational hazard.** Lifting and transferring patients causes real, career-ending injuries. Learn proper technique, use the lift equipment every time even when it is slower, and never let a busy shift talk you into a manual transfer.

**Short staffing means impossible ratios.** CNAs frequently describe being responsible for more patients than anyone could care for properly. That gap between what you should do and what you can do is what burns people out.

**Nights, weekends, and holidays.** Hospitals never close and new employees get the least desirable shifts.

**Emotional load.** You form attachments to patients, and some of them die. Nobody trains you for that part.

**The pay is low relative to the work.** This is the honest comparison: a [correctional officer](correctional-officer.md) starts near $33/hour and a [911 telecommunicator](911-telecommunicator.md) near $26. A CNA will start well below both. The case for this path is the ladder and the tuition benefit, not the starting wage.

**Verification gap:** the training program's cost and whether it is paid are unknown. If it charges tuition and does not pay you, it is a substantially weaker opportunity than it appears here, and it belongs in a different category.

## Where This Can Lead

This is a genuine ladder, and it is the main argument for the path.

**Inside healthcare:** CNA to patient care technician to LPN to RN is a well-worn route, and hospital tuition assistance is designed to move people along it. An RN in this region earns multiples of a CNA wage, and hospitals will help pay for the schooling of an employee who has proven reliable.

**Sideways into technical roles:** sterile processing, surgical technologist, orthopedic technician, phlebotomy, radiology technologist, respiratory therapy. Many require additional schooling, and employer tuition assistance applies.

**Non-clinical:** hospital operations, scheduling, health information management, patient access, and supply chain. A large hospital system is a large employer with many career directions that have nothing to do with bedside care.

The employee badge is the real asset. Internal candidates have advantages that external applicants do not, and tuition assistance is a benefit that pays for a career change while you are being paid.
