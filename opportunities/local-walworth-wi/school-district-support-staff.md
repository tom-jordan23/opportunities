## Overview

Every school district in the radius employs far more people than teachers, and most of those jobs require no credential to start.

Paraprofessional and instructional aide, custodian, food service, groundskeeper, bus aide, and clerical positions are all trained on the job. Districts hire locally, the commute is short, the schedule follows the school calendar, and — the part people miss — many district positions include **Wisconsin Retirement System** participation.

This is a modest-paying, low-drama, genuinely stable category of work. It will not make anyone wealthy. It is worth knowing about for three reasons: the commute is measured in minutes, the pension is real, and it is the most direct way to find out whether working with young people is something he wants to build a career around.

It also pairs naturally with [school bus driver](school-bus-driver-cdl.md) — several districts combine a driving route with an aide or custodial position to make a full-time job.

**A note on paraprofessionals specifically:** federal Title I rules impose an education standard for instructional aides in Title I programs — typically two years of college, an associate degree, or passing an approved assessment such as the ParaPro test. **The assessment route means no college is required**, which is the detail that keeps this in a hire-and-train category. Non-Title-I aide positions may not carry that requirement at all.

## What You'll Do

**Paraprofessional / instructional aide.** You support students in classrooms, often one-on-one or in small groups. A large share of these positions are special education roles supporting students with disabilities — which can mean academic support, behavioral support, personal care, and physical assistance. It is demanding work that requires patience and a genuine steadiness.

**Custodian.** Cleaning, floor care, minor repairs, snow removal, setup and teardown for events, and building security. Second shift is standard because the work happens after students leave. Head custodians manage building systems and are effectively facilities technicians.

**Food service.** Meal preparation, serving, cashiering, and kitchen sanitation. Hours are short and concentrated around meal service, which makes it a common part-time position.

**Groundskeeping and maintenance.** Mowing, athletic field maintenance, snow removal, and building repairs. Districts with multiple buildings run real maintenance departments.

**Clerical and administrative.** Front office, attendance, health room aide, library assistant, and registrar support.

The school calendar governs everything. Most positions are nine or ten months, with summers off — which is a genuine benefit and a genuine income gap.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+. High school diploma or equivalent for most positions. **Background check including fingerprinting and a check against child abuse and neglect registries** — this is thorough, as it should be.
- **Skills Needed:** None for most roles. **Paraprofessionals in Title I programs** must meet the federal standard: two years of college, an associate degree, **or a passing score on an approved assessment such as ParaPro.** The assessment is the no-college route and districts often help you prepare for it.
- **Physical Requirements:** Varies. Custodial and grounds work is physical. Special education paraprofessional roles can involve lifting, physical assistance, and occasionally managing physically aggressive behavior.
- **Time Commitment:** Most positions follow the school calendar — roughly nine to ten months. Full-time and part-time both common. Custodial positions are often year-round.

## Training Provided

**On the job, and district-funded.**

Districts train paraprofessionals in their specific programs, behavioral support strategies, crisis prevention and de-escalation (many districts certify staff in a formal program such as CPI), student privacy rules, and the needs of the specific students they support. Special education aides receive training related to individual education plans.

Custodial and maintenance staff learn building systems, floor care equipment, chemical safety, and boiler and HVAC basics — which is genuinely transferable to [facilities maintenance](industrial-maintenance-apprentice.md) work elsewhere.

Food service staff receive food safety training and typically a food handler certification.

**Where it gets interesting for advancement:** many districts offer tuition assistance or reimbursement, and there is a well-established route from paraprofessional to licensed teacher. Wisconsin has developed teacher apprenticeship pathways, and districts facing teacher shortages actively support aides who want to become teachers. **Ask about this specifically** — for a 21-year-old, an employer-supported route to a teaching license without accumulating debt is a substantial opportunity.

## The Money Reality

- **Pay:** **Not established and needs verification.** District support staff wages are set by local salary schedules, which are **public records** and usually posted on district websites or available on request. Paraprofessional and food service pay in Wisconsin is generally modest — expect the mid-teens per hour. Custodial and maintenance typically pays somewhat better. **These are among the lower-paying options in this category and that should be weighed honestly.**
- **Housing:** Your own. **This is the shortest commute available** — Walworth, Fontana, Big Foot, Delavan-Darien, Lake Geneva, and Elkhorn districts are all within minutes.
- **Startup Costs:** Effectively zero. Background check fees may apply and are often reimbursed.
- **Benefits:** **This is the real argument for these jobs.** Many district positions at sufficient hours include health insurance and **Wisconsin Retirement System** participation. A pension attached to a modest hourly wage is worth considerably more than the wage suggests, particularly starting at 21. **Confirm the hours threshold for benefit eligibility** — part-time positions often fall below it.
- **Hidden Costs:** **Summers are unpaid** for school-calendar positions. Nine months of income across twelve months of expenses is a real budgeting problem unless you arrange summer work.

## Hiring Pattern

**Heavy surge in July and August**, when districts staff up for the school year. That is when the most positions are open and hiring moves fastest.

Mid-year openings occur continuously, particularly for paraprofessionals and food service, because turnover in these roles is high.

**Districts in the radius:** Walworth J1, Fontana J8, Sharon J11, Big Foot Union High School, Delavan-Darien, Lake Geneva J1 and Badger High School, Elkhorn Area, Whitewater Unified, East Troy, Genoa City, Williams Bay, plus Janesville, Beloit, Burlington, and the Illinois-side districts in Harvard and Richmond.

That is a lot of separate employers, each hiring independently, all within a short drive.

## How to Get Started

1. **Check every nearby district's employment page directly.** Districts post on their own sites and on WECAN (the Wisconsin Education Career Access Network). Aggregators miss most of these.
2. **Apply in July or August** for the fastest results, though mid-year applications are worth submitting any time.
3. **Ask the benefits question first: "At how many hours per week does this position qualify for health insurance and Wisconsin Retirement System participation?"** This is the single most important variable and it separates a modest job from a genuinely good one.
4. **Ask about the ParaPro assessment** if you are interested in instructional aide work. Districts often pay the fee and provide preparation materials, and passing it opens Title I positions without college.
5. **Ask about tuition assistance and teacher apprenticeship pathways.** If working with students turns out to appeal to him, this is a route to a teaching license paid for while working.
6. **Consider combining a bus route with an aide or custodial position.** Several districts do this to create full-time work, and it comes with a free CDL. See [School Bus Driver](school-bus-driver-cdl.md).
7. **Ask what the district does about summers** — some offer summer school, maintenance, or grounds work to school-year staff.
8. **Apply to custodial and maintenance if the pay matters more than the setting.** It generally pays better than paraprofessional work and is more often year-round.

## The Hard Parts

**The pay is low, and that is the honest headline.** Paraprofessional and food service wages sit near the bottom of this entire category. A [correctional officer](correctional-officer.md) near $33/hour or a [911 telecommunicator](911-telecommunicator.md) near $26 earn multiples of a typical aide wage. The pension and the short commute are real, but they do not close that gap in the near term.

**Summers are unpaid.** This is a structural problem, not a detail. Plan for it or find a district position that runs year-round.

**Special education paraprofessional work is genuinely hard.** Supporting students with significant disabilities can involve personal care, physical assistance, and de-escalating aggressive behavior. Injuries to aides happen. Some of the work is emotionally heavy in a way people do not anticipate. It is also, for the right person, deeply worthwhile — but nobody should take it casually.

**Little authority, considerable responsibility.** Paraprofessionals are often responsible for a student's day without much say in decisions about that student.

**Part-time hours often fall below benefit thresholds**, which removes the main argument for the job. Verify before accepting.

**Custodial work is second shift**, which means evenings, and it is physical.

**Small-community dynamics.** Working in the district your family lives in means your work life and personal life overlap. That is fine for most people and uncomfortable for some.

**Limited advancement without a license.** The ceiling for support staff is head custodian, kitchen manager, or lead paraprofessional. Real advancement means becoming a teacher, which means a degree — hence the importance of asking about tuition support.

## Where This Can Lead

**The teacher pathway is the significant one.** Wisconsin districts facing teacher shortages actively support paraprofessionals who want to become licensed teachers, through tuition assistance and apprenticeship-style pathways. Starting as an aide at 21 and finishing a license in your mid-twenties with employer support and no debt is a legitimate route into a professional career with a pension.

**Facilities is the other ladder.** Custodian to lead custodian to head custodian to district maintenance and facilities director. Head custodians manage boilers, HVAC, and building systems — skills that transfer directly to [industrial and facilities maintenance](industrial-maintenance-apprentice.md) at higher pay.

**Food service:** cook to kitchen manager to district food service director, which is a real management position.

**Administrative:** school secretary, registrar, administrative assistant, and district office roles.

**Transportation:** combining with a bus route leads toward dispatcher and transportation director.

The Wisconsin Retirement System participation is the quiet asset. Years accumulated starting at 21 compound in a way that is easy to dismiss and hard to replace later.
