## What This Category Is

Every other category in this guide assumes you leave home. This one does not.

This category documents employers within roughly a 60-minute drive of Walworth, Wisconsin that hire people with no experience and train them on the job. You sleep in your own bed. You keep your existing car, phone, and support network. You get paid from the first day of training.

The trade-off is real: your options are limited to what this specific corridor happens to need. If nobody within an hour trains welders this year, you cannot make that appear. The upside is that your startup cost is close to zero — usually gas money, work boots, and a background check.

## What Qualifies for This Category

An opportunity belongs here only if it meets all four tests:

1. **Inside the radius.** Roughly 60 minutes of driving from Walworth, WI.
2. **Actively recruiting.** The employer is hiring now, or hires on a known repeating cycle.
3. **Trains on the job.** The employer teaches the skill after hiring. You do not pay tuition first.
4. **Paid from day one.** Training is compensated. Unpaid "training periods" do not qualify.

Roles that require you to buy a credential before anyone will interview you belong in `skills-and-trades/` instead. Some paths appear in both — the national document covers the trade, the local document covers who near Walworth will train you into it.

It is rarely worth it for general warehouse work that also exists in Delavan.

## The Four Mechanisms That Actually Deliver Training

"On-the-job training" is a phrase employers use loosely. Underneath it there are four genuinely different arrangements, with very different levels of protection for you. Knowing which one you are being offered is the single most useful thing in this document.

### 1. Registered Apprenticeship — the strongest option

A formal contract registered with the state. You are an employee earning a wage from day one. The wage rises on a defined schedule as you complete hours. Classroom instruction happens at a technical college, and the employer typically pays for it. You finish with a nationally recognized credential that transfers to any employer in the country.

Wisconsin runs roughly 10,000 registered apprentices across about 200 occupations, spanning construction, manufacturing, healthcare, and IT. Employment comes first — you need an employer willing to sponsor you before the apprenticeship exists.

- Wisconsin: [DWD Registered Apprenticeship — Applicants](https://dwd.wisconsin.gov/apprenticeship/ra/applicants.htm)
- Wisconsin: [Applicant FAQ](https://dwd.wisconsin.gov/apprenticeship/ra-applicant-faq.htm)
- Related instruction locally: Gateway Technical College (Racine/Kenosha/Elkhorn), Blackhawk Technical College (Janesville/Beloit)

**Why this is the best case:** the credential is portable, the wage schedule is written down, and the state is a third party to the agreement.

### 2. WIOA On-the-Job Training contracts — the underused option

This is the mechanism most people have never heard of, and it is worth understanding properly.

Under the federal Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act, a local workforce board can pay an employer to hire and train you — typically reimbursing a share of your wages during the training period. From your side you are a normal paid employee. From the employer's side, hiring an untrained person just got cheaper, which is exactly the friction that usually keeps beginners out.

The Walworth County Job Center states that assistance with on-the-job, classroom, and short-term training in high-demand careers is available, along with free career and skill assessments.

**This changes how you should job hunt.** Instead of only applying to postings that say "no experience needed," you go to the job center first, get established as an eligible participant, and then the board can approach employers on your behalf with funding attached. It is the difference between asking someone for a favor and bringing them a subsidy.

### 3. Employer in-house training with a certification clock

You are hired without the credential, trained internally, and required to earn a specific certification within a set window after hire — commonly six months. The employer usually pays for the exam.

**This is the pattern behind the cast tech role that started this search.** OrthoIllinois orthopedic technician postings indicate candidates need to be a CNA, CMA, ATC, or LPN *or* have the ability to obtain Registered Orthopedic Technician (ROT) certification within six months of hire. That "or" is the door. It means an uncredentialed person can be hired on aptitude and certify on the employer's clock.

**Read the clock carefully before signing.** Three questions decide whether this is a good deal:
- Who pays for the exam and the study materials?
- Is exam prep on paid work time, or your own evenings?
- What happens if you fail the exam — retake, or termination?

Also verify locally: this pattern was found in aggregated job-board summaries, not confirmed against a live OrthoIllinois posting. Their career center routes applications through an external UKG/UltiPro portal that was not readable. **Call HR at 815-398-9491 or email HR@OrthoIllinois.com and ask directly.** That call is itself step one of the application.

### 4. Youth Apprenticeship — NOT APPLICABLE (age 21)

Wisconsin's Youth Apprenticeship is a paid "earn while you learn" program for high school **juniors and seniors** — one or two years, minimum 450 hours per school year, combining paid work with related classroom instruction, coordinated through the school. Alliant Energy and We Energies run a Utilities Field Technician youth apprenticeship, though that particular one has been limited to participating Milwaukee County schools.

- [DWD Youth Apprenticeship — Applicants](https://dwd.wisconsin.gov/apprenticeship/ya/applicants.htm)
- [Program areas and occupational pathways](https://dwd.wisconsin.gov/apprenticeship/ya/skills-checklists.htm)

**This does not apply.** Wisconsin Youth Apprenticeship is limited to high school juniors and seniors, and he is 21. It is documented here only so the option is accounted for and can be ruled out. Use mechanisms 1 through 3.

### Public Service & People-Facing

Municipal and county employers train nearly everyone they hire, because the work is specific to the jurisdiction. They also tend to offer pensions, which almost nothing else in this list does.

Targets: Walworth County and Rock County government, City of Lake Geneva, Elkhorn, Delavan, Whitewater; police and fire cadet/explorer programs; correctional officer roles; public works and parks; transit; 911 dispatch; school district support staff.

- [Walworth County career opportunities](https://www.co.walworth.wi.us/445/Career-Opportunities)

Dispatch deserves attention: it is trained from zero, pays reasonably, and never appears on anyone's list of career options.

Lake Geneva's tourism economy also produces year-round hospitality roles at the Grand Geneva and Geneva National — worth documenting, but be clear-eyed that hospitality training rarely produces a portable credential.

## How to Verify "Actively Recruiting"

Job postings expire in weeks. This guide's normal convention — recheck anything older than 12 months — is far too slow here.

**The rule for this category:** document the *employer's hiring pattern*, not the individual posting. "Kikkoman runs continuous 2nd/3rd shift production hiring and accepts walk-in applications" stays true for years. "Requisition 4471 is open" is dead by next month.

Every document in this category carries a **Hiring Pattern** section stating whether the employer hires continuously, seasonally, or in occasional cohorts, and how to check current status in one step.

**Re-verification cadence:** pay figures and hiring status every 3 months. Everything else follows the project's 12-month standard.

## Local Scam & Caution Notes

The scam patterns in `CLAUDE.md` were written for programs that take your money up front. Local employment has a different threat profile.

| Pattern | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Staffing agency churn** | A "direct hire with training" that is actually a temp placement. You are the agency's employee, not the plant's. Training is thin, the assignment ends without notice, and there is no path to permanent. | Ask directly: "Am I employed by you or by an agency?" Ask what share of temps convert to permanent, and after how long. |
| **Training repayment agreements** | You sign an agreement to repay training costs if you leave within 12-24 months. Common in trucking, spreading to healthcare. Legal, but it converts a job into a debt. | Read it before signing. Ask for the dollar figure and the exact term. A registered apprenticeship never does this. |
| **Certification clock with no support** | "Certify within 6 months" where you pay the exam fee, study unpaid, and are fired if you fail. | Ask who pays, whether prep is on the clock, and what happens on a failed attempt. Get it in writing. |
| **Fake local postings** | Scrapers repost expired jobs, and fake listings impersonate real local employers to harvest personal data. | Apply through the employer's own site or in person. Never send an SSN or bank details before a written offer. |
| **Unpaid "working interview"** | A shift you work for free to "show you can do it." | Illegal in most cases. Decline. |
| **Commission-only sales as "training"** | Pitched as a management training program. Actually door-to-door commission work with high turnover. | If pay is not hourly or salaried, it does not belong in this category. |

The Elkhorn Job Center is a useful check on all of this. Staff there know which local employers churn.

## Suggested First Week

Concrete steps, each doable in the next seven days.

1. **Go to the Walworth County Job Center in person** — 400 County Rd H, Elkhorn, M-F 8:00-4:30, (262) 741-5180. Say plainly: "I want on-the-job training. What OJT contracts do you have with employers right now?" Ask about the free skill assessment. This one visit does more than a week of online applications.
2. **Call OrthoIllinois HR** — 815-398-9491 or HR@OrthoIllinois.com. Ask whether they hire cast techs without prior certification and train to ROT, and what the six-month certification terms are.
3. **Walk into Kikkoman** — N1365 Six Corners Rd, Walworth. Ten minutes away. Applications accepted in person. Confirm current shift openings, pay, and the hiring bonus.
4. **Call Mercyhealth about the Nursing Assistant Training Program** — ask whether it costs anything, whether training hours are paid, and whether completing it leads to a guaranteed position.
5. **Create a Job Center of Wisconsin account** and set alerts for Walworth, Rock, Racine, and Kenosha counties.
6. **Check the Walworth County and Rock County job portals directly** — county jobs are rarely surfaced by aggregators.
7. **Age 21 note:** this clears the minimum for a CDL and for serving alcohol, and meets the age floor for every opportunity documented in this category.
