# Firefighter / EMT (Paid-On-Call to Career)

> **Status:** Draft
> **Last Verified:** Not yet fact-checked — drafted 2026-08-26
> **Fact Checker:** Pending
> **Hiring Pattern:** Paid-on-call recruitment is nearly continuous. Full-time career openings are infrequent and competitive.

## Overview

Walworth County's fire service is built on a mix of full-time, part-time, and **paid-on-call** staff. That structure is the opportunity, because paid-on-call is a door that is almost always open.

You join a local department part-time, they put you through Firefighter I training, you respond to calls, and you get paid for your time. It is not a full-time income. It is a way to earn a credential, find out whether you actually want this work, and build the record that makes you competitive for a career position later.

The full-time end of the ladder is real money in this county. The **Elkhorn Area Fire Department** — which covers the City of Elkhorn plus the Towns of Geneva, Lafayette, and Sugar Creek, the largest municipal fire response area in Walworth County — advertises a **minimum starting salary near $59,958 annually** for Firefighter/EMT on a 24-hours-on, 48-hours-off rotation.

Wisconsin's entry training requirement is lighter than most people assume. Under **SPS 330**, one recognized option is a **WTCS-approved entry-level firefighter course of 60 hours, delivered in two 30-hour modules.** Sixty hours is a few weekends, not a semester.

## What You'll Do

Modern fire departments run far more medical calls than fires, and this is the single most common surprise for new firefighters. Expect the majority of your calls to be emergency medical: cardiac events, falls, breathing difficulty, overdoses, and car crashes. If you do not want to do medical work, this is the wrong career.

The fire work itself: structure fires, vehicle fires, brush fires, alarm calls, extrication from crashes using hydraulic tools, hazardous materials response, and water rescue depending on the department. Lake Geneva-area departments deal with lake and ice rescue.

Between calls, career firefighters maintain equipment, inspect hydrants, conduct fire prevention inspections and public education, train constantly, and keep the station running. Paid-on-call members typically train on scheduled evenings and respond from home or work when paged.

The 24-on/48-off schedule for career positions is genuinely unusual — you work a full day and night, then have two days off. People either love it or find it impossible to build a life around.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+. High school diploma or equivalent. Valid driver's license; a CDL is sometimes required or trained for. Background check and drug screen.
- **Skills Needed:** None required. Departments state that experience is not necessary, only a desire to help others. **EMT certification is usually required or must be obtained**, and for career positions paramedic certification is increasingly expected.
- **Physical Requirements:** Substantial and non-negotiable. Carrying heavy equipment, working in protective gear that adds significant weight and heat load, climbing ladders, and dragging hose and people. A physical ability test is standard. Cardiac events are a leading cause of firefighter death, so fitness is an occupational safety matter, not vanity.
- **Time Commitment:** Paid-on-call means scheduled training plus availability for pages — often a minimum monthly call or training percentage. Career positions are typically 24/48 rotations.
- **Residency:** Paid-on-call departments usually require you to live or work within a set response distance. **Check this first**, because it determines which departments you can even join.

## Training Provided

**Departments train you, and it is generally paid or department-funded.**

**Entry-level:** Under SPS 330, a WTCS-approved **entry-level firefighter course of 60 hours in two 30-hour modules** is a recognized minimum training option. DSPS notes SPS 330 does not require formal "certification" for that minimum option. **Firefighter I** has been the Wisconsin minimum since January 1, 2010 for most roles, along with National Incident Management System requirements.

**Progression:** Firefighter I to Firefighter II, then Hazmat Operations, Fire Officer I, driver/operator certifications, and specialty rescue disciplines.

**EMS:** EMT-Basic is typically required or obtained early, and many departments pay for it. Paramedic training is a longer commitment and a significant pay increase — departments often sponsor it for career staff.

**Where training happens:** Gateway Technical College, Blackhawk Technical College, Madison College, and the Wisconsin REACT Center all deliver fire service training. WTCS offers a Fire Science technical diploma explicitly aimed at preparing people for paid-on-call and volunteer positions.

**The key question to ask every department:** does the department pay for Firefighter I and EMT, or are you expected to arrive with them? Both models exist in this county.

## The Money Reality

- **Pay — career:** Elkhorn Area Fire Department advertises a **minimum starting salary near $59,958.08 annually** for Firefighter/EMT on a 24/48 rotation. That is a real, documented local figure and it compares well with everything else in this category.
- **Pay — paid-on-call:** Typically an hourly or per-call rate for training and responses. **Not established locally and it varies widely.** Treat paid-on-call as supplemental income and credential-building, not as a living.
- **Municipal benchmark — VERIFIED:** the **City of Whitewater 2026 Salary Resolution** (effective 1/1/2026) lists **Fire Inspector EMT/Firefighter in Grade I at $24.16 minimum / $28.39 mid / $32.62 maximum** per hour — annualized, $50,257 to $67,846. The same schedule places **Assistant Fire & EMS Chief** in Grade N ($34.73-$46.89/hr), **EMS Chief** in Grade M ($32.30-$43.60/hr), and **Fire Chief** in Grade Q ($43.18-$58.29/hr). That gives you the whole municipal fire ladder with figures attached.
- **Housing:** Your own. Paid-on-call generally requires living within a response area, which for a Walworth resident means the local department.
- **Startup Costs:** Low if the department funds training. Turnout gear is department-supplied — never buy your own. Costs arise if you must self-fund EMT or Firefighter I.
- **Benefits:** Career positions include health insurance and **Wisconsin Retirement System** participation under the protective occupation category. Paid-on-call typically has no benefits.
- **Hidden Costs:** Self-funded EMT or paramedic training if unsponsored. Time — training nights and call response are a real demand on your schedule.

## Hiring Pattern

**Paid-on-call recruitment is close to continuous.** Wisconsin's paid-on-call fire service has a well-documented staffing shortage, and departments across Walworth County recruit constantly.

**Career openings are infrequent and competitive.** A full-time position at Elkhorn or Lake Geneva may open once every few years, and candidates who are already paid-on-call members with certifications in hand have a substantial advantage.

**Departments in the radius:** Walworth, Fontana, Williams Bay, Lake Geneva, Elkhorn, Delavan, Darien, Sharon, Genoa City, East Troy, Whitewater, Burlington, plus Janesville, Beloit, Racine, and Kenosha for larger career departments.

Walworth County has published planning work on the future of fire and EMS in the county, which is worth reading — it describes the staffing pressures that create these openings.

## How to Get Started

1. **Walk into your local fire station and ask about joining as paid-on-call.** Walworth, Fontana, and Williams Bay are all within minutes. Volunteer and paid-on-call departments are approachable in a way that few employers are — go on a training night.
2. **Ask the decisive question: "Does the department pay for Firefighter I and EMT training, or do I need to bring them?"** This determines the actual cost of entry.
3. **Confirm the residency and response-time requirement** before applying.
4. **Ask what the minimum commitment is** — required training nights and call response percentage.
5. **Get EMT certification early.** It is the most in-demand credential in the fire service and it opens ambulance service employment independently. See [EMT Certification](../skills-and-trades/emt-certification.md).
6. **Ask the Walworth County Job Center** whether WIOA funds EMT or fire service training — both commonly appear on the Eligible Training Provider List.
7. **Get in genuine physical condition now.** The physical ability test eliminates candidates, and fitness is a real safety issue in this job.
8. **Watch career postings at Elkhorn, Lake Geneva, Janesville, Beloit, Racine, and Kenosha** while building paid-on-call experience.
9. **Consider paramedic school** once you are certain. It is the clearest path to a competitive career application.

## The Hard Parts

**It is mostly medical calls, not fires.** People join to fight fires and spend their careers on cardiac arrests and falls. If EMS work does not appeal to you, this career will disappoint you.

**Career positions are genuinely competitive.** Departments receive many applications for few openings, often from candidates who already hold paramedic certification and years of paid-on-call experience. Expect the path to take years.

**Paid-on-call is not an income.** It is a credential and a foot in the door. You need another job alongside it.

**The 24/48 schedule is a lifestyle, not a shift.** You miss a third of all holidays, birthdays, and family events permanently.

**The health risks are serious and well documented.** Cardiac events are a leading cause of firefighter death. Occupational cancer rates are elevated due to exposure to combustion products. Departments have improved decontamination practices for exactly this reason, and following them matters.

**You will see traumatic things**, including pediatric calls, and in a small community you will know the people involved. That last part is specific to rural and small-town fire service and it is harder than people expect.

**Sleep disruption.** Overnight calls, whether from the station or from your own bed.

**The physical standard is real** and it does not become optional with age.

## Where This Can Lead

**The paid-on-call to career route is the main path**, and it works. Certifications plus call experience plus departmental relationships make you a credible career candidate.

Progression within a department: firefighter to driver/operator to lieutenant, captain, battalion chief, and chief. Fire service leadership is promoted internally.

**Paramedic is the highest-value move.** It substantially improves career hiring odds and opens ambulance service and hospital-based EMS employment independently.

Specialties: fire inspector and investigator, hazmat technician, technical rescue, dive and water rescue (relevant on Geneva Lake), and training officer.

Adjacent: private ambulance services, industrial fire brigades at large plants, emergency management, and fire protection engineering and sprinkler inspection.

The protective-occupation pension for career positions is a substantial long-term asset.
