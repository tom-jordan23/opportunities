# IT Service Desk Technician Apprentice

> **Status:** Draft — a public-sector pay benchmark VERIFIED 2026-08-26
> **Last Verified:** 2026-08-26 (municipal IT pay verified; apprentice wages still unknown)
> **Fact Checker:** Pending
> **Hiring Pattern:** Employer-dependent. Fewer sponsors than the trades, but the region has hospitals, manufacturers, colleges, and county governments that all run internal IT.

## Overview

Wisconsin has a **registered apprenticeship for IT Service Desk Technician.** That sentence deserves attention, because almost nobody knows it exists.

The standard advice for getting into IT without a degree is to pay for a bootcamp or buy certifications yourself and hope. Wisconsin has an alternative: an employer hires you, pays you an hourly wage, pays you **while you attend classroom instruction**, and the program includes completion of the **CompTIA A+ certification.**

That is the same structure as an electrician's apprenticeship, applied to technology work. You earn while you learn, you finish with a recognized industry certification, and you carry no tuition debt.

This is the only path in this category that leads toward office and technology work rather than physical or shift work. If your son is more interested in computers than in machinery, this is the document to read.

The honest caveat: IT apprenticeship sponsors are less numerous than manufacturing or trades sponsors. Finding an employer is harder here. The offsetting advantage is that far fewer people are asking.

## What You'll Do

You are the first line of technical support for an organization's employees.

Tickets come in — a laptop will not connect, a password is locked out, a printer is offline, an application is throwing an error, a phone will not sync. You triage, diagnose, and either fix it or escalate it. You do this by phone, by chat, by remote session, and by walking to someone's desk.

The technical range at this level: account administration and password resets, workstation setup and imaging, software installation and licensing, basic network troubleshooting, printer and peripheral support, mobile device management, and hardware repair or replacement.

A large part of the job is not technical at all. You explain things to people who are frustrated and not technically inclined, you document what you did clearly, and you do it patiently and repeatedly. **Service desk work is a customer service job with a technical subject.** People who succeed at it are patient communicators who happen to be good with computers. People who are technically strong but dismissive of users do badly.

The work is indoors, seated, at a computer, generally on a normal schedule — though organizations that run continuously, like hospitals, may need off-hours coverage.

## Requirements

- **Age/Eligibility:** 18+. High school diploma or equivalent. Background check standard, and more thorough for healthcare, government, or financial employers because of the data access involved.
- **Skills Needed:** No formal requirement, but you need genuine basic computer literacy and evident curiosity. Having built a PC, run a home server, troubleshot a family member's laptop, or taught yourself something on YouTube all count as real signals and you should mention them.
- **Physical Requirements:** Minimal. Seated work, occasional lifting of computers and monitors, crawling under desks. **The most accessible option in this category for anyone with a physical limitation.**
- **Time Commitment:** Full-time, generally daytime business hours. Apprenticeship term varies by sponsor — confirm.

## Training Provided

**A Wisconsin registered apprenticeship with paid related instruction.**

- **Paid classroom time.** Apprentices are paid their regular hourly wage during related instruction.
- **CompTIA A+ certification is a completion requirement** — a genuinely recognized, portable, entry-level IT credential that normally costs several hundred dollars in exam fees plus study materials.
- **Transition to Trainer** is also listed as a completion requirement, meaning you learn to train others.
- **Defined wage progression** as you complete hours and coursework.
- **No tuition debt.**

Beyond the formal program, on-the-job learning is substantial: the organization's ticketing system, Active Directory or equivalent identity management, its network layout, and its application stack. That practical exposure is what employers actually hire for at the next level, and it is difficult to get any other way.

**Related instruction** is delivered through the Wisconsin Technical College System. Gateway Technical College and Blackhawk Technical College serve this region and both run IT programs.

Note that Wisconsin also lists a **Software Developer apprenticeship**, which is a longer and more selective path but exists for anyone whose interest runs toward programming rather than support.

## The Money Reality

- **Pay — a verified benchmark, and it corrects what this document originally said.** The **City of Whitewater 2026 Salary Resolution** (effective January 1, 2026) places **IT Support Technician in Grade K: minimum $27.94, mid-point $32.82, maximum $37.71 per hour** — annualized, $58,107 to $78,444.

  That is the same grade the city assigns to its **Water Operator and Wastewater Operator**, and its minimum sits **above** Walworth County's Highway Maintenance Worker entry rate of $25.08. An earlier draft of this document asserted that IT service desk work pays "meaningfully below" the trades and public safety at entry. **For public-sector IT in this region, that is not correct** — it is competitive with them.

  Two caveats. IT Support Technician is a step above a pure entry-level help desk role, so a first job may start lower. And private-sector help desk and managed-service-provider pay in this region is generally below the municipal figure. **Apprentice starting wages remain unknown** and still need to be obtained in writing.
- **Housing:** Your own.
- **Startup Costs:** Effectively zero. No tools, no boots, no tuition. The A+ exam is a program requirement rather than a personal expense. This ties with pharmacy technician as the lowest-cost entry in the category.
- **Benefits:** Employer-dependent. Hospital, county government, technical college, and large-manufacturer IT departments generally offer good benefits, and public employers may include **Wisconsin Retirement System** participation.
- **Hidden Costs:** Continuing certification costs later in your career, if you pursue them independently. Otherwise minimal.

## Hiring Pattern

Employer-dependent, and the sponsor pool is thinner than in manufacturing. But the radius contains a lot of organizations that run internal IT departments:

- **Hospital systems** — Mercyhealth, Advocate Aurora, Beloit Health System, SSM Health, OSF. Healthcare IT is large, well-funded, and always hiring.
- **County and municipal government** — Walworth County, Rock County, and city governments all run IT departments.
- **Educational institutions** — UW-Whitewater (roughly 25 minutes away and a substantial IT employer), Gateway Technical College, Blackhawk Technical College, Rock Valley College, and K-12 school districts.
- **Large manufacturers** — Generac, Snap-on, SC Johnson, Kikkoman and others all run internal IT.
- **Managed service providers** — regional IT firms that support many small businesses. These hire entry-level technicians frequently, give very broad exposure, and are known for high workload and high turnover.

**UW-Whitewater is worth specific attention.** Universities are large IT employers, are close, and often have entry-level support positions plus tuition benefits.

## How to Get Started

1. **Read the DWD IT Service Desk Technician apprenticeship page** so you can name the program precisely when you talk to employers. Most hiring managers will not know it exists, and being the person who explains it is an advantage.
2. **Call the DWD Bureau of Apprenticeship Standards at (414) 216-8417** and ask which employers sponsor IT apprenticeships in Walworth, Rock, Racine, or Kenosha county. This is the fastest route to a sponsor.
3. **Contact Gateway Technical College's apprenticeship department at (262) 564-2954** and ask the same question for IT specifically.
4. **Apply to hospital, county, university, and school district IT help desk openings directly**, whether or not they mention apprenticeship. Get hired first; ask about the apprenticeship after.
5. **Check UW-Whitewater's employment page** for IT support and help desk positions.
6. **Apply to regional managed service providers.** Demanding, but they hire beginners and the breadth of exposure in one year is unusual.
7. **Start studying for CompTIA A+ now, free.** Professor Messer's video course is free and widely used. Even without taking the exam, being able to discuss the material makes you a credible candidate immediately — and it costs nothing but time.
8. **Build something you can point to.** A home lab, a rebuilt computer, a home network with a firewall. Employers hiring at this level are looking for demonstrated curiosity, and a real project beats a résumé line.
9. **Ask the Walworth County Job Center** about WIOA funding for IT training — IT is a named priority sector for the board.

## The Hard Parts

**Service desk work is emotionally repetitive.** You will reset the same password for the same person for the fourth time. You will be blamed for problems you did not cause. Users can be condescending, and being the person who fixes printers is not glamorous inside an organization.

**The tickets never stop.** The queue is always there. There is no version of this job where you finish.

**It can be a dead end if you let it.** Service desk is a genuine on-ramp, but people do stay there for a decade. Escaping requires deliberately acquiring skills and certifications beyond the daily work, on your own initiative. Nobody will push you.

**Entry pay is uneven, and it depends heavily on who employs you.** Public-sector IT in this region is competitive — Whitewater's IT Support Technician grade starts at $27.94/hour. Private help desk and managed service provider work generally pays less. A correctional officer still starts higher at $33.66. But the original claim that IT "starts well below" the trades holds only for the private-sector entry roles, not the municipal ones. **Target public-sector and hospital IT.**

**Fewer apprenticeship sponsors.** You may not find one, and may need to take a regular help desk job instead and pursue certifications yourself.

**Managed service provider burnout is well documented.** Great learning, heavy workload, high turnover.

**Certification treadmill.** Technology changes and credentials expire. Continuous learning is not optional in this field, which suits some people and exhausts others.

**On-call and after-hours** for organizations that run continuously, particularly hospitals.

## Where This Can Lead

This has the highest long-term ceiling of anything in this category, reached without a degree — but it requires you to keep moving.

The standard progression: service desk to desktop support to systems administrator or network administrator, then to engineer or architect roles. Each step is a substantial increase, and senior infrastructure and security roles pay very well.

**Specializations worth aiming at:** networking (Cisco CCNA and beyond), cloud infrastructure (AWS or Azure certifications), **cybersecurity** (high demand, strong pay, and Security+ is a natural next step after A+), systems administration, and database administration.

Also reachable from here: healthcare IT (a large specialty in its own right, and the hospitals are right here), IT project management, and technical training.

**Public sector IT deserves a mention** in this specific region. A county or university IT career includes Wisconsin Retirement System participation, stable hours, and tuition benefits — a combination that is rare in private technology work.

### Scam Check
- [ ] Red flags: none for the state apprenticeship program or for hospital, government, and university employers.
- [ ] Yellow flag: **IT bootcamps and "job guarantee" programs.** This is the most scam-adjacent sector in this entire category. Coding and IT bootcamps have a documented history of inflated placement statistics, and income share agreements can be predatory. The registered apprenticeship route costs nothing — exhaust it, and free A+ study, before paying anyone tuition.
- [ ] Yellow flag: **certification mills.** Only recognized certifications (CompTIA, Cisco, Microsoft, AWS) carry weight. Unrecognized "IT certificates" are worthless.
- [ ] Scam risk level: **Low** for apprenticeship and direct employment; **High** for the private bootcamp market
